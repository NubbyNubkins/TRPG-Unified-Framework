PART A. The six coloured far-side parts as real particles
========================================================================================================
  What they are (44.2): with the colourless weak pair (the Higgs) they fill the far side's 10 parts. Six = a coloured triplet of charge 1/3,
  spin 0 (three colours x particle/antiparticle). This is exactly the Higgs pair's coloured partner in the 1974 unified models.
  Its effect on the running: hypercharge +0.0667, weak 0, strong +0.1667
  without it: sin^2 = 0.2076 (-10.2%), meeting at 6.8e+14 GeV
  with it from   1e+03 GeV: sin^2 = 0.2038 (-11.9%), meeting at 1.0e+15 GeV
  with it from   1e+06 GeV: sin^2 = 0.2047 (-11.4%), meeting at 9.2e+14 GeV
  with it from   1e+10 GeV: sin^2 = 0.2060 (-10.9%), meeting at 8.0e+14 GeV
  with it from   1e+13 GeV: sin^2 = 0.2070 (-10.5%), meeting at 7.2e+14 GeV
  It makes the gap WIDER at every mass. Reason: it slows the strong force's weakening (strong +1/6) and touches
  the weak force not at all, so the weak and strong lines meet later, where hypercharge has already passed. Same for ANY number of copies.
  ATTACK from the other side (could they be spin 1/2 instead?): a coloured triplet of spin 1/2 adds hypercharge 2/15 and strong 2/3:
    from 1 TeV: sin^2 = 0.1930 (-16.5%). Also wider.
  And they cannot be light in any case: a coloured charge-1/3 piece that touches quarks and leptons makes the proton decay. Super-Kamiokande
  (proton lasts > 2.4e34 years) puts it above ~1e11 GeV even with the smallest (electron-sized) couplings. So as real particles: FAIL twice.
  What closes the gap instead, for the record: things carrying the WEAK charge between the Z and the meeting. Required change: b_weak - b_strong
  must grow by about 0.88 from 1 TeV (pure weak-charged additions). The far side has no such part beyond the Higgs pair. FAIL.

========================================================================================================
PART B. One joint rule for the up and down ladders, tilt = angle between them
========================================================================================================
  B1. The shared-state rule. TRPG reads each ladder as a cone around one axis, and the two ladders as hung from one shared state (47.4).
      Written as matrices: each ladder = (all-ones matrix: the shared state, every family touching every family equally) + a small break.
      This is the 'democratic' form (Harari, Haut & Weyers 1978; Koide; Fritzsch & Plankl 1990). Both ladders share the SAME all-ones part;
      they differ only in the break. The tilt then comes out; nothing is fitted to it. Break used: (-d, +d, e) on the diagonal (the simplest that
      gives three different masses; Fritzsch & Xing 1996). Three masses fix three numbers per ladder.
      4 sign choices solve. Results (|V_us|, |V_cb|, |V_ub|), measured (0.225, 0.04183, 0.003732):
        up signs (1, -1), down signs (1, -1):  0.1793  0.0092  0.00632
        up signs (1, -1), down signs (-1, 1):  0.1711  0.0152  0.00657
        up signs (-1, 1), down signs (1, -1):  0.2659  0.0151  0.00525
        up signs (-1, 1), down signs (-1, 1):  0.2579  0.0103  0.00542
      best: 0.2659 (1.18x), 0.0151 (0.36x), 0.00525 (1.41x)
  B2. The tilt as the plain angle between the two ladders (each ladder as the arrow (sqrt m1, sqrt m2, sqrt m3)):
      angle = 4.49 degrees; Cabibbo angle 13.0, V_cb angle 2.40, V_ub angle 0.21. Matches none (and it uses the masses' own sizes).
  B3. The cone form of the same idea: the two cones' axes tilted by the measured tilt. an earlier test C5 showed the same-axis start gives zero tilt;
      putting the measured tilt in by hand gives back the measured tilt and no masses: it relates, it does not produce. Not a rule.

========================================================================================================
Summary
========================================================================================================
  A: the six coloured far-side parts as real particles widen the strength gap and would break the proton. FAIL. The strengths need weak-charged
     states between the Z and the meeting energy, which the main line does not have. Force groups stays PARTIAL.
  B: the shared-state (democratic) joint rule is the right SHAPE (both ladders from one shared part, the tilt comes out unfitted) but its
     numbers fail; the plain angle fails. Quark masses stay PARTIAL.

========================================================================================================
ATTACK on B1: give the shared-state rule its most general diagonal break (one free number per ladder) and let it FIT the tilt
========================================================================================================
  best over 2 free numbers (one per ladder) and all signs: |V_us| 0.2164 (0.96x), |V_cb| 0.0162 (0.39x),
  |V_ub| 0.00359 (0.96x)   (break shifts +0.000, -0.001)
  Verdict: even with a free number per ladder (break shifts from -0.3 to +0.3) the turn to the third family stays 0.39x too small. The first and
  outer turns can be fitted, the middle one cannot. The shared-state rule with a diagonal break is RULED OUT, not only unfitted.
