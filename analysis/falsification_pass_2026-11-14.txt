PART 1. FORCE GROUPS: can 'all three forces follow from the bag rules' be shown to fail?
====================================================================================================
Needed (measured): 12 carriers in three blocks that do not mix: 8 (2 neutral) | 3 (1 neutral) | 1 (1 neutral). Rank 4 in total.
The 4x4 (Pati-Salam) version needs rank 5 (15 + 3 + 3 carriers).

1a. Every reading of 'a symmetry of the inner space', applied the SAME way to each number system the bag rules allow
    reading                                                       complex  quaternions   octonions
    A. turns that keep the multiplication (automorphisms)       0 (rank 0)  3 (rank 1) 14 (rank 2)
    B. the same, with one direction picked (the perspective)    0 (rank 0)  1 (rank 1)  8 (rank 2)
    C. turns that keep sizes (the literal content of rule 2)    1 (rank 1)  6 (rank 2) 28 (rank 4)
    D. the same, keeping the unit fixed                         0 (rank 0)  3 (rank 1) 21 (rank 3)
    E. the same, respecting the perspective's complex structure  1 (rank 1)  4 (rank 2) 16 (rank 4)
    E'. the same as E, also keeping the unit fixed              0 (rank 0)  1 (rank 1)  9 (rank 3)
    F. multiplying by unit elements, left side only (closure)   1 (rank 1)  3 (rank 1) 28 (rank 4)
    G. multiplying by unit elements, left and right (closure)   1 (rank 1)  6 (rank 2) 28 (rank 4)
    Wanted in ONE row: 1 (rank 1) for hypercharge, 3 (rank 1) for the weak force, 8 (rank 2) for colour.
    -> No row has them. Colour's 8 (rank 2) appears only in row B on the octonions. The weak force's 3 appears in rows A, D, F on the
       quaternions. an earlier test took colour from row B and 'left and right' from row G: two different readings of rule 3.
    Row E in detail: the self-interacting parts have 3 and 15 carriers: quaternions give SU(2) x U(1) (4 carriers, 2 neutral: the measured
    electroweak count) and octonions give SU(4) x U(1) (the 4x4 colour group of an earlier test plus one). Row E is the only single reading that gets the
    weak block's count right, but it gives 16 colour-side carriers, not 8, one U(1) too many, and still needs two separate inner spaces. A lead, not a result.
    Also: the 4x4 colour group (15 carriers, rank 3) cannot sit inside the octonion's symmetries of row A (14, rank 2). So 'octonions give colour'
    (an earlier test, row B) and 'the lepton is the 4th colour' (an earlier test, which is row E) are two different structures, and both have been in use.

1b. Can ONE octonion inner space hold colour and the weak force side by side?
    Row A: the octonion's symmetries have rank 2; the measured forces need rank 4. Impossible, whatever the details.
    Row C (most generous, 28 turns, rank 4): turns that commute with colour's 8: 2, and they commute with each other: True.
    -> next to colour there is room for 2 single (U(1)-type) carriers and no room for the weak force's 3. And rank 4 < 5: the 4x4 group cannot fit at all.

1c. So the weak force needs a second number system (the quaternions) next to the octonions. Does putting them together keep rule 1?
    complex x quaternions : (1 + i e1) x (1 - i e1) = 0.0   <- two non-zero things multiply to nothing: a hole in the glass
    complex x octonions   : (1 + i e1) x (1 - i e1) = 0.0   <- two non-zero things multiply to nothing: a hole in the glass
    quaternions x octonions: (1 + i(x)e1) x (1 - i(x)e1) = 0.0
    The published octonion routes to the weak force (Dixon; Furey) work inside exactly these products and build particles from
    projectors P with P x (1 - P) = 0. They need the holes that rule 1 forbids.
    The only way left: keep three SEPARATE inner spaces at every point (complex, quaternion, octonion), never multiplied together.
    That keeps rule 1, but it is an added assumption (why three spaces, each once?), and row by row above it still needs a different
    reading of 'symmetry' for each space: unit multiplication for the first two (row F: 1 and 3) and row B for the octonions (8).
    Noted, not used: with a perspective picked in the quaternions too, row B leaves 1 carrier there and 8 in the octonions. That is the
    pattern of the two forces that stay unbroken in nature (electromagnetism and colour). Suggestive; it is not a derivation of the weak force.
    -> RESULT: 'rules 1-4 give SU(3) x SU(2) x U(1)' is FALSE as stated. Rules 1-2 allow one number system at a time;
       the octonions give colour and nothing else that commutes with it except single carriers. The weak force and hypercharge are NOT derived.

1d. What survives the attack
    Colour: any of 5 new random perspectives leaves (carriers, neutral) = [(8, 2)] -> SU(3), three colours. Survives.
    Uniqueness from counting: 8 carriers with 2 neutral is only SU(3); 4 with 2 neutral is only SU(2) x U(1). That is the
    Killing-Cartan classification, a theorem. It survives, but its inputs (the counts) are MEASURED, not derived from the bag rules.
    Consistency (anomalies) of the 16 particles per generation: sum Y = +0.0e+00, sum Y^3 = +2.3e-16, weak-weak-Y = +0.0e+00, colour-colour-Y = +0.0e+00: all zero. Passes.

1e. Left-handedness: does 'nothing extra' (rule 6, the minimal 4x4 content) survive the measured masses?
    The minimal 4x4 Higgs content (one bidoublet) forces, at the right side's scale: down-type quark mass = charged lepton mass, generation by generation,
    and up-type quark mass = neutrino Dirac link (Pati & Salam 1974).
      bottom / tau     : predicted 1, measured 0.73
      strange / muon   : predicted 1, measured 0.23   (off by 4.4x)
      down / electron  : predicted 1, measured 2.33   (off by 2.3x)
    And with rule 5 (neutrino link = electron family's sizes) the same content forces top = tau: measured top/tau at that scale is about 50.
    -> The minimal content is CONTRADICTED by data and by rule 5. At least one more Higgs piece is required (the (15,2,2); Georgi & Jarlskog 1979).

    Force running redone for each consistent content (one loop). Needs: right side >= heaviest partner (1.0e11-1.3e11 GeV); proton > 2.4e34 yr (Super-K).
    content                                               right side     unify  proton (yr)   verdict
    minimal (bidoublet + right triplet)                  10^11.74  10^16.05  10^ 36.0     OK | handedness put in: the content itself is lopsided
    left-right symmetric (+ left triplet)                10^13.70  10^15.21  10^ 32.5     proton likely 69x too fast | handedness explained (spontaneous)
    minimal + (15,2,2) light                             10^11.48  10^16.36  10^ 37.0     OK | fixes the mass relations; handedness put in
    symmetric + (15,2,2) light                           10^13.70  10^15.28  10^ 32.7     proton likely 45x too fast | fixes the mass relations; handedness explained
    minimal + (15,2,2) at the top scale                  10^11.74  10^16.05  10^ 36.0     OK | fixes the mass relations; handedness put in
    symmetric start, left triplet lifted to the top scale 10^11.74  10^16.05  10^ 36.0     OK | handedness explained; needs one parity-odd field (Chang, Mohapatra & Parida 1984)
    -> Every version that passes the proton and partner tests either puts handedness in by hand or adds a field beyond 'nothing extra'.
       The fully symmetric versions are in tension with the proton limit at this rough one-loop level (a factor of about 3 in the unification
       scale; heavy-particle thresholds could move it either way, so this is 'likely too fast', not a firm exclusion).
       'Nothing left to assume beyond rule 6' is FALSE: rule 6 itself, read literally, is contradicted.

1f. The gauge principle 'follows from rule 2': do the theorems' conditions hold inside the bag rules?
    Counter-example: the octonion algebra at a point satisfies rules 1 and 2 exactly and contains no time, no relativity and no probabilities.
    So rules 1-2 cannot by themselves force a LOCAL symmetry; a global one satisfies them equally well.
    The theorems (Weinberg 1964; Llewellyn Smith 1973; Cornwall, Levin & Tiktopoulos 1974) assume special relativity, quantum mechanics
    (probabilities add to 1 in TIME) and, for heavy carriers, weak coupling. None of these is among rules 1-7.
    -> RESULT: the gauge principle is a theorem of relativity + quantum mechanics, which TRPG inherits. It is NOT a consequence of rule 2.
       Honest repair: list relativity and quantum mechanics as inherited starting rules. Then rule 3 can be dropped; the count of rules does not shrink.

1g. A condition the 4x4 picture brings with it (found while attacking it)
    Breaking the 4x4 group makes magnetic monopoles of mass about 2.8e+13 GeV. If the universe was ever hotter than the break (5.5e+11 GeV)
    after it happened, the standard estimate (Zeldovich & Khlopov 1978; Preskill 1979) gives a monopole density of about 69x the critical
    density (allowed: under 0.12). So TRPG needs: never hotter than ~5e11 GeV after the break, yet at least ~1e11 GeV for the matter excess
    (falsification_matter_excess, E2). A window of about 5x. Rough estimate; it is a condition, not a failure.

PART 2. QUARK MASSES: the colour rule, the bottom mass and charm
====================================================================================================
Lepton family (measured): scale 313.851 MeV, lean r = 1.414209 (sqrt2 = 1.414214), twist = 0.2222248 (2/9 = 0.2222222)

2a. The colour rule taken as an exact rule, against each measurement's own error bar
    strange            predicted     92.28   measured    93.50 +- 0.80   off -1.31%  =  -1.5 sigma
    charm              predicted   1359.57   measured  1273.00 +- 4.60   off +6.80%  = +18.8 sigma
    bottom             predicted   4197.47   measured  4183.00 +- 7.00   off +0.35%  =  +2.1 sigma
    top (direct, GeV)  predicted    173.26   measured   172.57 +- 0.29   off +0.40%  =  +2.4 sigma
    All four together: chi2 = 366 for 4 numbers: probability 5e-78. Without charm: chi2 = 12.3 for 3: probability 0.006.
    -> As an exact rule it FAILS: charm alone is 19 sigma out, and even without charm the other three sit 1.5-2.4 sigma on the wrong side.
       The bottom cannot be kept while charm is dropped: both come out of the same two numbers (3 x scale, 3 x twist).

2b. Is there ANY single, consistent way of defining quark masses in which the rule holds?
    definition                                                         Q(-s,c,b)  lean r twist/(2/9) scale/lepton  Q(c,b,t)
    mixed, as used in an earlier test (s at 2 GeV, c and b at own scale, t direct)    0.6748  1.4314       2.925        2.911    0.6692
    own scale for all (t = 162.5 GeV running)                             0.6748  1.4314       2.925        2.911    0.6626
    all at 2 GeV (ratios from lattice QCD)                                0.6981  1.4794       2.519        3.159         -
    all at the bottom's scale                                             0.6981  1.4794       2.519        2.624         -
    all at M_Z (Huang & Zhou 2021)                                        0.6981  1.4795       2.518        1.781    0.7200
    pole masses (c 1.67, b 4.78, t 172.57 GeV)                                 -       -           -            -    0.6485
    THE RULE SAYS                                                         0.6667  1.4142       3.000        3.000    0.6667
    At ANY one common energy the shape is fixed by two lattice ratios: Q(-s,c,b) = 0.6981 +- 0.0002, against 2/3 = 0.6667: 143 sigma out.
    In the mixed convention: scale/lepton = 2.9115 +- 0.0060 (-15 sigma from 3); twist/(2/9) = 2.9246 +- 0.0051 (-15 sigma from 3); lean = 1.4314 +- 0.0017 (+10 sigma from sqrt2).
    -> No consistent definition makes the rule hold. It comes closest only when each quark is quoted in a different convention,
       and even there every one of its three numbers (3, 3, sqrt2) is many sigma away. RESULT: the colour rule FAILS as a law.
       It stays what Rivero called it in 2011: a numerical observation, good to a few per cent.

2c. Charm: can any standard definition rescue it?
    charm wanted from (b, t), 45-degree rule   :  1353.0 MeV
    charm wanted from the colour rule          :  1359.6 MeV
    charm wanted from (s, b), 45-degree rule   :  1372.1 MeV
    charm in standard definitions: 986 (running, at 3 GeV), 1100 (at 2 GeV), 1273 +- 5 (at its own scale), 1548 (half the J/psi), 1670 +- 70 (pole).
    The wanted 1353-1372 matches none of them. A running mass equals 1360 at an energy of about 1.07 GeV, but nothing in TRPG picks that energy;
    since some definition can be found for ANY number between 0.99 and 1.67 GeV, 'a different definition' is an escape that cannot fail. Rejected.
    -> RESULT: charm FAILS (17 to 19 sigma). The heavy-quark 45-degree relation is approximate (6-8%), not a law.
       The 'one leftover up-side factor' lead of an earlier test is withdrawn: the top value in that comparison is computed FROM charm, so it is not a second witness.

2d. Every link of the quark chain, each against its own error bar
    top = full Higgs link (y_t = 1)                predicted   174.10 +-  0.00  measured   172.57 +- 0.29 GeV  off   +0.9%   +5.3 sigma   FAILS
    top from measured (c, b), 45 degrees           predicted   168.60 +-  0.28  measured   172.57 +- 0.29 GeV  off   -2.3%   -9.9 sigma   FAILS
    charm from measured (b, t), 45 degrees         predicted  1352.96 +-  3.00  measured  1273.00 +- 4.60 MeV  off   +6.3%  +14.6 sigma   FAILS
    strange from measured (c, b), 45 degrees       predicted    84.49 +-  0.44  measured    93.50 +- 0.80 MeV  off   -9.6%   -9.9 sigma   FAILS
    strange/down from the Cabibbo cell (1/Vus^2)   predicted    19.88 +-  0.14  measured    20.36 +- 0.27      off   -2.4%   -1.6 sigma   PASSES
    bottom from the colour rule                    predicted  4197.47 +-  0.00  measured  4183.00 +- 7.00 MeV  off   +0.3%   +2.1 sigma   borderline

    Why the chain looked better before: it fed its own too-high charm (1353) into the strange link, which then gives 91.8 MeV. With the measured
    charm the same link gives 84.5. The good strange value rested on the wrong charm.
    -> Strictly: the Cabibbo link passes. Everything that runs through the 45-degree rule for quarks fails or is borderline. The bottom stays an INPUT.

====================================================================================================
PART 3. THE 2/9 TWIST
====================================================================================================
3a. As a measured rule for the charged leptons (their masses are directly measurable, so there is no definition problem)
    all three masses measured: Q = 0.6666645 +- 0.0000051 (2/3 = 0.6666667: -0.4 sigma);  twist = 0.2222248 +- 0.0000063 (2/9 = 0.2222222: +0.4 sigma)
    -> each rule on its own agrees with data inside the tau mass's error bar.

3b. The sharper test: BOTH rules exact at once (45 degrees AND 2/9). Then two mass ratios are predicted with nothing free.
    electron/muon : predicted 0.0048362841   measured 0.0048363317 +- 1.1e-10   off -9.83 parts per million = -447 sigma
    tau/muon      : predicted 16.8180467      measured 16.8176918 +- 0.0008518       off +21.1 parts per million = +0.4 sigma
    twist needed by the electron/muon ratio if 45 degrees is exact: 0.22222204710; 2/9 = 0.22222222222; difference -1.75e-07 (-0.79 ppm)
    (with that twist the tau comes out at 1776.969 MeV; measured 1776.93 +- 0.09)
    lean needed instead if 2/9 is exact: r = 1.414213261 (sqrt2 = 1.414213562), i.e. Q = 0.666666525; tau then 1776.967 MeV.
    Both alternatives fit the tau mass, so the data cannot say WHICH of the two is inexact, only that they are not both exact.
    -> RESULT: 'exactly 45 degrees and exactly 2/9' is RULED OUT by the electron/muon mass ratio, by about 8-9 parts per million
       (hundreds of sigma, because that ratio is known to 0.02 ppm). At least one of the two is approximate at the 1e-6 level.
       So 2/9 is a very good approximation (better than 1 in 100,000), not an exact number of nature in this form.

3c. Does the rule hold at other energies (running masses)?
    at M_Z: Q = 0.667824, twist = 0.221129 (2/9 = 0.222222; off -0.492%). The rule is a statement about the directly measured masses only.

3d. The ring reading (two paths round a ring of 3 seats: (1/3)(2/3) = 2/9; Shulga 2026). What does it predict that could fail?
    ring sum = 0.2222222 (2/9 = 0.2222222): the arithmetic is right.
    (i) Unit. A ring's natural unit is the full turn. 2/9 of a turn = 1.3963 rad; the measured twist is 0.2222 rad = 0.03537 of a turn.
        The reading only works if 'one ring = one radian', which nothing gives. For leptons it therefore predicts nothing: it was matched to the number.
    (ii) Its one extension, quarks = 3 x 2/9 = 0.6667: measured 0.6499 +- 0.0011 in the mixed convention (-15 sigma); 0.5597 at any common energy. FAILS.
    (iii) Neutrinos (TRPG's masses, lightest root sign +1): lean 1.0690, twist 0.3664 = 2/9 +0.1441. Not 2/9, and the ring gives no reason for the difference.
    (iii) Neutrinos (TRPG's masses, lightest root sign -1): lean 1.4120, twist 0.4801 = 2/9 +0.2579. Not 2/9, and the ring gives no reason for the difference.
    -> RESULT: the ring reading is a way of writing 2/9, not an explanation. It has a free unit, predicts nothing new for leptons,
       and its only extension fails. It is WITHDRAWN as an explanation. The twist stays one measured number.
