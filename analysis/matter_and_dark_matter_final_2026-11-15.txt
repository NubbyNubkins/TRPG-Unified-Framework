2. Hot majorons: when the heat itself makes them (this is what kills the hot start)
==============================================================================================================
  A majoron once in contact with the heat keeps 2.60e-03 per unit entropy. All of the dark matter then needs m_J = 168 eV,
  and it is a thermal relic of that mass. Lyman-alpha needs a thermal relic above 5.3 keV (Irsic et al. 2017, 95%) or 5.7 keV (Irsic et al. 2024):
  168 eV is 32x too light. FAIL. At m_J = 8.6 keV thermal majorons would be 51x too much dark matter. FAIL either way.
  With the earlier numbers (130 eV): hot share 77% (an earlier test quoted 73%; same statement).
  How large must f be so that a heat of temperature T_max makes under 1% hot majorons (the wave still being all of the dark matter, angle 1)?
   T_max = 1e+09 GeV (partners 1.6e+10, 1.0e+11): f > 1.7e+10 GeV, where m_J = 1.8e+06 eV and the heaviest partner's link y_3 = 8.5e+00
   T_max = 1e+10 GeV (partners 1.6e+10, 1.0e+11): f > 3.6e+11 GeV, where m_J = 8.2e+00 eV and the heaviest partner's link y_3 = 3.9e-01
   T_max = 1e+11 GeV (partners 1.6e+10, 1.0e+11): f > 5.4e+11 GeV, where m_J = 1.7e+00 eV and the heaviest partner's link y_3 = 2.6e-01
   T_max = 3e+11 GeV (partners 1.6e+10, 1.0e+11): f > 5.4e+11 GeV, where m_J = 1.7e+00 eV and the heaviest partner's link y_3 = 2.6e-01
  -> A hot start (T_max ~ 1e11 GeV, what thermal leptogenesis needs) is compatible with majoron dark matter only if f is above about 5e+11 GeV,
     where the majoron is a light wave (m_J about 1.7 eV or less). At f = 6.3e10 to 1e11 GeV it is not: hot share at f = 6.3e10, T_max = 1e11: 52x the whole dark matter (must be under a few % of it).
     (Cross-section known to a factor of a few; f_min moves as its 1/7 power along the dark-matter line.)

  2b. Freeze-in instead of the wave (majorons leaking slowly out of the heat; Hall et al. 2010; Frigerio, Hambye & Masso 2011):
   They are born with momentum about 2.5 to 3.2 x temperature, so Lyman-alpha (thermal relic > 5.3 keV) needs m_J > 12 to 16 keV
   (same range as Heeck & Teresi 2017: 12 to 16 keV).
   Through the Higgs (h -> J J by mixing with the settling piece): needs an effective link 3.3e-09, i.e. (Higgs-to-settling link)/(settling self-link) = 2e+09 at f = 6.3e10.
   That takes two more numbers, both extremely small, and drags the Higgs mass up unless tuned. Not in the main line: NOT PURSUED (allowed only by adding numbers).
   Through the partners (N N -> J J in a heat at T ~ M_2 = 1.6e10): right amount for f about 1e+12 GeV. But there the wave overfills unless the starting angle is under 0.041, and the matter excess is the thermal one (2.6 to 3x short). FAIL as a joint solution.

8. Honest count and verdict

  Data: 2 numbers (eta_B, Omega_DM h^2).
  Free numbers in the working version (C): f, m_J, the starting angle (dark matter: 3 numbers, 1 relation);
     the handover X and the CP asymmetry eps (matter excess: eps needs the perspective and the 3 unmeasured phases; 1 relation). At least 5 for 2 data.
  So neither number is derived. Both are ALLOWED, in one consistent cold-start picture that passes every limit checked.
  What changed against earlier tests:  (1) the 1.4 keV is 8.7 keV with the exact oscillator (and it is a relation, not a mass);
     (2) gravity's leak at n = 8 does not give the dark matter at f = 6.3e10 once its 8 valleys are counted;
     (3) no X-ray line; (4) with a cold start 6% of random perspectives reach the matter excess (0% with a hot start); rule 5 read literally still fails, by 1.9x;
     (5) the hot start is not excluded at f > 5e+11 GeV, but then the matter excess is 2.6 to 3x short;
     (6) the settling piece decays at least 91% to majorons: as the single source it works only with a long cold stretch, and then m_J = 16 eV (corner).
