0. The two pictures as exact statements, written before any number is looked at
====================================================================================================
  CONE.    sqrt(mass of rung k) = size x (1 + opening x cos(twist + k x 120 degrees)).  Three rungs, three numbers: size, opening, twist.
           C1  each ladder is a cone                       (any three masses are: no content by itself)
           C2  all ladders share the leptons' opening, 45 degrees
           C3  the two quark ladders share ONE opening (whatever it is)
           C4  all six quarks sit on one 45-degree cone   (Kartavtsev 2011)
           C5  both quark ladders are cones about the SAME axis -> then the weak force's tilt between them is exactly zero
  SPIRAL.  each turn climbs: rung k+1 = rung k x one set factor.
           S1  each ladder climbs by equal steps           (middle^2 = bottom x top)
           S2  the up ladder is the down ladder with every step squared (one spiral, two pitches)
           S3  the step is set by the tilt: down ladder steps = leak^2, up ladder steps = leak^4   (Froggatt & Nielsen 1979; Wolfenstein 1983)
           S4  the two ladders are ONE spiral seen from its two ends: same opening, twist mirrored (the spiral of an earlier test)
           S5  the leptons' exact cone, wound into a spiral: same opening and twist, plus one climb factor per rung
  9 statements. A pass needs the measured value inside its own error bar, at one energy, for BOTH ladders.

2. The cone: where each ladder sits (size, opening, twist)
====================================================================================================
  leptons      size    313.85 +/- 0.01 MeV   opening 45.000 +/- 0.000 deg   twist 12.733 +/- 0.000 deg   relationship/self 1.0000 +/- 0.0000
  down ladder  size    434.65 +/- 5.76 MeV   opening 48.150 +/- 0.015 deg   twist 5.705 +/- 0.016 deg   relationship/self 1.2465 +/- 0.0013
  up ladder    size  20479.14 +/- 99.87 MeV   opening 52.147 +/- 0.018 deg   twist 3.043 +/- 0.022 deg   relationship/self 1.6556 +/- 0.0021
  C1  each ladder is a cone: TRUE for any three masses, so it says nothing by itself. The content is in the three numbers above.
  C2  all share the leptons' 45 degrees: leptons 44.9999 +/- 0.0002 (holds). Down 48.15 (+213 sigma). Up 52.15 (+405 sigma). FAIL.
  C3  the two quark ladders share one opening: 48.15 against 52.15: apart by 174 sigma. FAIL.
  C4  all six quarks on one 45-degree cone: sum of masses / (sum of square roots)^2 = 0.6865 +/- 0.0015; a 45-degree cone needs 0.6667: +13 sigma. FAIL.
  C5  both ladders are cones about the SAME axis: then one turn of 120 degrees maps each ladder to itself, the two mass patterns can be
      sorted by the same three directions, and the weak force changes up-type into down-type with NO leak between families.
      (an earlier test found the same.) Measured leaks: 0.2243, 0.041, 0.0037. Zero is the right first guess (the leaks are small) and FAILS strictly
      (the first by more than 100 sigma).
      So the tilt between the ladders IS the amount by which the two cones do not share an axis. A cone alone cannot give it.
  Seen in the numbers, going leptons -> down -> up: the opening widens (45.0, 48.2, 52.1 deg) and the twist unwinds
      (12.73, 5.71, 3.04 deg). Two orderings of three things line up like this by chance about 1 time in 3. A trend, not a law.
  -> The cone is exact for the leptons and for nothing else. For quarks it is a way of writing three masses, with openings that match no simple value
     (an earlier test: 5/4 and 5/3 fail) and that differ between the ladders.

3. The spiral: does each turn climb by a set factor?
====================================================================================================
  S1  leptons      middle^2 / (bottom x top) =   12.295 +/- 0.001  (equal steps need 1)     second step / first step, in powers: 0.529 +/- 0.000  (need 1)
  S1  down ladder  middle^2 / (bottom x top) =    0.370 +/- 0.006  (equal steps need 1)     second step / first step, in powers: 1.332 +/- 0.007  (need 1)
  S1  up ladder    middle^2 / (bottom x top) =    1.956 +/- 0.075  (equal steps need 1)     second step / first step, in powers: 0.892 +/- 0.006  (need 1)
      FAIL for all three as an exact rule. But look at the last column: the quark ladders are 1.3 and 0.9, the leptons 0.5.
      The quarks are much NEARER a spiral of equal steps than the leptons are. The leptons are the cone; the quarks lean toward the spiral.
  S2  up ladder = down ladder with every step squared: power needed on the first step 2.081 +/- 0.023, on the second 1.394 +/- 0.004; need 2 and 2.
      One power for both steps would also do: they differ by 30 sigma. FAIL.
  S3  the step is set by the tilt (leak = 0.2243). Steps DOWN each ladder, measured against the rule:
         down / strange    0.05020 +/- 0.00083    leak^2 = 0.05031    measured / rule = 1.00    power of the leak that fits: 2.00
         strange / bottom  0.01857 +/- 0.00006    leak^2 = 0.05031    measured / rule = 0.37    power of the leak that fits: 2.67
         up / charm        0.00198 +/- 0.00007    leak^4 = 0.00253    measured / rule = 0.78    power of the leak that fits: 4.17
         charm / top       0.00386 +/- 0.00005    leak^4 = 0.00253    measured / rule = 1.53    power of the leak that fits: 3.72
      One of four lands (down / strange, the Cabibbo link that already passes: Gatto, Sartori & Tonin 1968). The other three are off by
      factors of 0.4 to 1.5. The fitted powers (2.0, 2.7, 4.2, 3.7) are near whole numbers and not on them. An old, real, rough pattern. As a law it FAILS.
  S4  one spiral seen from its two ends: needs the same opening (C3: FAIL) and a mirrored twist. Twists: down 5.71 +/- 0.02, up 3.04 +/- 0.02 deg.
      Mirror through the axis would need them to add to 60: they add to 8.7. Mirror in the turning sense leaves a cone's three masses
      unchanged, so the two ladders would be EQUAL (up = down, charm = strange, top = bottom). FAIL both ways.
      What survives of the earlier spiral here: the two ladders ARE the two ends of one state (the quark pair joins the up partner one way and
      the down partner the other way, 45.3). That fixes which links exist. It does not fix their sizes.
  S5  leptons' cone wound into a spiral, down ladder: climb factor needed on the first step 0.310 +/- 0.003, on the second 1.789 +/- 0.003; must be equal. FAIL.
  S5  leptons' cone wound into a spiral, up ladder: climb factor needed on the first step 1.564 +/- 0.027, on the second 3.924 +/- 0.027; must be equal. FAIL.

4. Attacks on my own tests
====================================================================================================
  (a) Did the choice of energy decide the outcome? The down ladder's cone and its steps do not change with energy under the strong force (all
      three masses run alike), so C2, C3, S1 and S3 for the down ladder are the same at every energy below the top. The up ladder was taken
      at the top's own energy; an earlier test followed both to 10^16 GeV and they drift in opposite directions. No energy rescues C2, C3 or C4.
  (b) Was a signed root missed? With the lightest root taken negative the opening only widens, so C2 to C4 still fail:
      leptons: with a negative lightest root, opening 46.52 deg.
      down ladder: with a negative lightest root, opening 50.78 deg.
      up ladder: with a negative lightest root, opening 52.38 deg.
  (c) Nine statements were tested. One sub-test of S3 landed, and it was already known (the Cabibbo link). Nothing new passed, so there is
      no question of a lucky hit to discount.

5. Summary
====================================================================================================
  CONE   : exact for the leptons (45.000 degrees, a part in a million). For quarks it is only a way of writing three masses. FAIL as a rule.
  SPIRAL : the right rough shape for quarks (each rung climbs by a large factor; quarks are nearer equal steps than leptons; the steps are
           roughly whole powers of the leak). FAILS as an exact rule in every form tested.
  LEARNED: (1) leptons are the cone, quarks lean to the spiral: the two are different shapes, which is why the lepton rule never carried over.
           (2) The tilt between the ladders is exactly the failure of the two cones to share an axis. (3) The down ladder's shape does not
           depend on the energy at which it is looked at, so it is a definite object to explain: opening 48.15 degrees.
  NOT MOVED: bottom, charm, strange and top stay measured. Scorecard unchanged.
