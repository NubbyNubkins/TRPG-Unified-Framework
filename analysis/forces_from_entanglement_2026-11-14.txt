1. Entanglement already lives on the bag's number systems
====================================================================================================
  Two two-state systems (qubits) = 4 complex amplitudes = a PAIR of quaternions; all states of size 1 form the 7-sphere.
  The quaternion map (a, b) -> 2 a b* has two 'extra' components. For 2,000 random states they equal the entanglement (Wootters' concurrence)
  to within 0.0e+00; for 500 unentangled states they are zero to within 1.8e-16.
  -> The quaternion structure of a pair IS its entanglement (Mosseri & Dandoloff 2001). Three qubits do the same with a pair of octonions
     (Bernevig & Chen 2003), and the ladder stops at the octonions for the bag's own reason: the next system has holes.

3. 'Inner gravity': a particle moving in an inner direction behaves exactly as a charged particle (Kaluza 1921; Klein 1926)
====================================================================================================
  Energy function of a free particle in the 5-dimensional shape = that of a charge q in a field A, where q = momentum round the inner ring.
  Checked for 200 random fields: largest difference 9.2e-14. Charge conservation = the ring's symmetry; the gauge freedom = relabelling the ring.
  What this gives for free: the gauge principle (a force for each direction the inner space can be turned without changing sizes).
  Where the PURE version fails (two published results):
    a) a particle whose charge is inner motion must weigh about e/sqrt(16 pi G) = 5.2e+17 GeV; the electron weighs 5.1e-4 GeV. Off by 1e+21.
    b) handed (left-only) matter cannot come out of pure geometry on a smooth inner space (Witten 1983).
  -> 'Everything, matter included, is gravity of the inner space' FAILS. What survives is the force side: inner turns that keep sizes are forces.
     The same conclusion comes from emergent gravity directly: where gravity comes from entanglement, an exact symmetry cannot stay 'global';
     it must come with a force field (Harlow & Ooguri 2019; proven in the anti-de Sitter setting, expected in general).

4. The turns of the 3x3 relationship matrix, for each number system the bag allows
====================================================================================================
  number system      all turns   + face one way  + stand at one spot    both (rule 4)
  complex           8 (rank 2)       8 (rank 2)           4 (rank 2)       4 (rank 2)
  quaternions      21 (rank 3)       9 (rank 3)          13 (rank 3)       5 (rank 3)
  octonions        52 (rank 4)      16 (rank 4)          36 (rank 4)      12 (rank 4)
  Measured: 12 carriers, 4 of them neutral, in blocks 8 | 3 | 1.

  Octonion case in detail: 12 carriers, 4 neutral. Self-interacting part 11, plus 1 that commutes with everything.
    turns that leave the colourless entries alone : 8 carriers, 2 neutral   -> SU(3), colour
    turns that commute with colour               : 4 carriers, 2 neutral   -> 3 self-interacting (SU(2), the weak force) + 1 single (U(1), hypercharge)
    the two blocks do not mix (largest cross-commutator 9.3e-16); together 12 = all 12.
  -> ONE meaning of symmetry (turns that keep how relationship matrices combine: the same meaning an earlier test used for colour), on ONE object
     (the 3x3 octonion relationship matrix of an earlier test), with rule 4 read as 'stand at one spot AND face one way', gives exactly 8 | 3 | 1.
     This is the published result of Dubois-Violette & Todorov (2018) and Krasnov (2019), reproduced here from scratch.
  Note the complex row: the weak block (4 carriers, 2 neutral) is already there for an ordinary complex 3x3 relationship matrix, the kind
  rule 5 gives the leptons. The weak force comes from the MATRIX (the relationships); colour comes from the octonion's extra directions.

5. What the weak force acts on, and with what charges
====================================================================================================
  The 16 link components (8 complex states) carry the single carrier's charge in the ratio [1.0, 3.0] with multiplicities [12, 4] (real components).
  Weak 'spin' of every link state: j(j+1) = [0.75] -> all are doublets (j = 1/2). Colour: 12 components feel it (3 colours x 2 x 2), 4 do not.
  The colourless doublet's charge is 3.000 times the coloured doublet's in size.
  -> One colourless weak doublet and one coloured weak doublet, with charges 3 : 1 in size. That is the LEFT-HANDED lepton pair (neutrino,
     electron; hypercharge 1/2 in size) and quark pair (up, down; 1/6). The relative sign is only a naming (a weak pair and its antiparticle pair
     are the same object here); with the usual naming it is -1/2 and +1/6, and 3 x (1/6) = 1/2 is exactly what step 6 needs.
     Only doublets appear: the weak force acts on every state in this space.

====================================================================================================
6. Quantum consistency: what must the partners of those doublets be?
====================================================================================================
  Doublets alone: weak-weak-charge = 0 (fine); colour-colour-charge = 1/3; charge cubed = -2/9; so the doublets alone are NOT consistent.
  Adding weak-singlet partners (two coloured, one colourless) with unknown charges, the three consistency conditions have the solutions: [{y_d: -1/3, y_e: -1, y_u: 2/3}, {y_d: 2/3, y_e: -1, y_u: -1/3}]
  -> Unique (up to which coloured one is called 'up'): +2/3, -1/3 and -1. Those are the measured right-handed up, down and electron.
     A neutral partner for the neutrino (TRPG's hidden partner) is allowed and, with its own mass, fixes the charges completely (Babu & Mohapatra 1989).
  Emergent gravity forbids exactly conserved 'global' numbers. Baryon-minus-lepton number: sum over 15 states = -1 (cannot be a force); with the
  hidden partner (16 states) = 0 (can), and its own mass then breaks it. Either way the hidden partner is the consistent choice. TRPG has it.

7. Attacks
====================================================================================================
7a. Does the 3x3 octonion matrix obey rule 1 (no holes)?
    Nothing squares to nothing: trace of X o X is at least 1.47 x |X|^2 over 2,000 random matrices (it is the sum of squares of all entries).
    By contrast complex x octonions has (e1 + i e2)^2 = 0.0: something that squares to nothing.
    Two DIFFERENT slots do multiply to zero (they do not overlap), as in every matrix TRPG already uses. Rule 1, as 'no relationship squares to
    nothing and squares never cancel', holds (Jordan, von Neumann & Wigner 1934). The entries are single octonions: no joined number systems.
7b. The right-handed force of the 4x4 picture
    All turns of the 3x3 octonion matrix: 52 carriers, only 4 neutral. The 4x4 picture with a right-handed force needs 5 neutral.
    -> It cannot fit. This route and the right-handed force (W_R, the 'right side's scale') exclude each other. TRPG has to choose.
       Choosing this route removes the earlier break 2 entirely: no 4x4 symmetry means no forced 'down = electron' mass relations, no lopsided
       content, no proton-decay tension, no monopoles. It also removes the right side's carriers that made the hidden partners (see the matter-excess note).
7c. Still assumed, stated plainly
    i)   'Stand at one spot' is an added reading of rule 4. Without it the count is 16 (rank 4), not 12.
    ii)  The three slots here are 'the spot + the two members of a weak pair'. In an earlier test the three placements are the three generations.
         Whether they can be both is NOT shown (Boyle 2020 proposes that the three choices of spot are the three generations).
    iii) Why the right-handed partners are weak singlets, and not mirror doublets, is argued (paired states freeze out heavy; Georgi 1979), not derived.
    iv)  That a symmetry must be a force: a theorem of relativity + quantum mechanics, and of emergent gravity where that is under control.
    v)   The remaining 40 turns: no carriers if the spot and facing are facts of observation (as in an earlier test); 40 heavy carriers if they are dynamical.
7d. Does it matter which spot and which facing direction?
    4 other spots inside the plane being faced: carriers [12, 12, 12, 12] (same forces every time, as with colour in an earlier test).
    4 spots outside that plane: carriers [2, 2, 2, 2]. The spot must lie in the view: 'you stand inside what you are looking at'.
7e. What else is in the matrix besides the weak pairs? (the entries not linked to the spot)
    10 components: charge sizes [0.0, 0.3333] with multiplicities [4, 6]; weak spin j(j+1) = [0.0, 2.0] with multiplicities [7, 3].
    -> a neutral weak triplet (3), a coloured weak singlet of charge 1/3 in size (6: the size of the right-handed DOWN quark's charge), and 1 neutral singlet.
       Suggestive only; not used for anything here.
7f. A sharper version that could fail: if the whole matrix had ONE strength, the three forces would be tied together.
    One strength for all 12 carriers predicts the weak mixing number sin^2(theta_W) = 0.750 at the energy where the strong and weak strengths meet.
    Running the measured strengths up (one loop), they meet at 9.8e+16 GeV, where the measured-and-run value is 0.437.
    -> FAILS: the route gives the KINDS of force and their charges, not their strengths. The three strengths stay measured inputs.
7g. Can the three slots ALSO be the three generations, with each generation 'standing at' its own slot? (Boyle 2020's proposal, plain form)
    standing at slot 1: 12 carriers, of which colour 8 (the same 8 every time) and weak block 3 + 1
    standing at slot 2: 12 carriers, of which colour 8 (the same 8 every time) and weak block 3 + 1
    standing at slot 3: 12 carriers, of which colour 8 (the same 8 every time) and weak block 3 + 1
    The weak blocks of slot 1 and slot 2 together span 6 directions (3 would mean 'the same weak force'); they do not commute (size 0.08).
    All three together close into 8 carriers (rank 2): a force that turns the generations into each other.
    Counting: the matrix has 24 link components in all; three generations of weak pairs need 3 x 16 = 48.
    Measured: ONE weak force, the same for all three generations (tested to about 0.1-0.2% in tau, muon and pion decays), and 3 weak carriers, not 8.
    -> FAILS in the plain form: the same 3x3 matrix cannot give both the weak force (slots = spot + pair) and the three generations (slots = generations).
       The published way round this doubles the matrix with complex numbers (Boyle 2020), and complex x octonions has holes (7a): not allowed by rule 1.
       So if the matrix gives the forces, the earlier 'at most 3 generations because octonion matrices stop at 3x3' is no longer available; and the reverse.
