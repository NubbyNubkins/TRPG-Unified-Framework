1. The three-way product on the link states
====================================================================================================
  The link states seen from the observer's spot: two octonions (16 numbers). Written with an outside i they are 16 complex states.
  Two-way product: x times y. Three-way product: {x, y, z} = (x.y*).z + (z.y*).x - (x.z).y*, where y* is y's mirror image (Jordan 1933; Loos 1977).
  It never leaves the link states (leak 0e+00) and obeys the standard consistency law for three-way products (error 5e-13).
  Two-way: the entry a = e1 + i e2 squares to nothing (size 0e+00). That is the hole that ruled out joined number systems in earlier tests
  Three-way: the same entry gives {a, a, a} = 4 a. Not nothing.
  For every state: the weight of 'x with its own mirror' is positive (all 16 weights = 12); smallest |{x,x,x}| / |x|^3 in 20,000 random states: 1.03.
  -> With a three-way product NOTHING combines with itself into nothing. The doubled link space has no three-way holes (Loos 1977: it is 'positive').

6. Attacks
====================================================================================================
6a. Is this the weak force of an earlier test? No. These 12 share 9 turns with the earlier 12 (the 8 colour turns and 1 more), and only 9 of them keep the
    matrix's own two-way product. The weak force here is a symmetry of the three-way product, not of the matrix. the earlier weak force made every link
    state a pair; this one makes half of them pairs and half singles, as measured.
6b. Other readings. Keeping BOTH states exactly, with the overall phase allowed, also gives 12 turns, but the single carrier's charges are then {0.0: 2, 1.0: 6, 2.0: 6, 3.0: 2}:
    it counts how many colour switches are on (0, 1, 2 or 3). That is the 'sets of three' and it is a real pattern (Georgi & Glashow 1974; Furey 2015),
    but as a force it is inconsistent: its charges do not sum to zero (24). Keeping both only as directions gives 13 or 14 turns: an extra carrier that
    is not seen. Only 'one exactly, one as a direction' gives the measured 12. It is the natural reading of rule 4, and it is still a choice.
6c. Facing the other way (u -> -u) picks the other half-related pair of states. Its 12 turns share 9 with the first 12, and its charges have the same sizes: True.
6d. Two UNRELATED states instead of half-related ones: 16 turns = 15 self-interacting + 1 single; 4 neutral. That is a four-colour force (lepton as fourth colour), not the measured forces.
    So the two states must be 'half-related'. Half-related means: each is a relationship partner of the other, neither is the other's mirror.
6e. The two-way hole is still there in the entries (e1 + i e2 squares to nothing). This route needs rule 1 to be read for three-way combinations.
    Whether the joined number systems rejected in an earlier test would also pass a three-way reading has NOT been tested.
6f. Nothing extra: exactly 16 states and 12 carriers. No mirror states: the 16 are not their own mirror image, so matter is one-handed here without
    the hole argument of an earlier test (provided spin uses this same i, as in an earlier test).
6g. Not new mathematics: the 16 are the 'spinor' of Georgi 1975 and Fritzsch & Minkowski 1975, without their extra force carriers; the three-way
    form is Loos 1977 and Baez 2026. What is TRPG's is the reading: spot exactly, facing as a direction, and no three-way holes.
