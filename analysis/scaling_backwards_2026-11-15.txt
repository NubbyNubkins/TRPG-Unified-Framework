  Range over the whole climb: leptons 45.054 to 45.054; down 47.721 to 48.150; up 52.151 to 52.312 degrees.
  down ladder reaches 45 degrees: never, between the top's energy and the Planck energy
  up ladder reaches 45 degrees: never, between the top's energy and the Planck energy
  Why: the strong force pulls all quark masses down by almost the same factor as the energy rises, so ratios inside a ladder barely move.
  The opening of a ladder depends only on its mass ratios, so it hardly changes with energy. Scaling up does not turn quarks into cones.
  At the far-side scale: b/tau = 0.909, s/mu = 0.309, d/e = 3.273; top link 0.607

  start at strong = weak : 0.3 -> 0.746, 0.5 -> 0.989, 1 -> 1.199, 2 -> 1.278, 3 -> 1.295, 5 -> 1.305, 10 -> 1.310
      a large start lands at about 1.310 (top mass 228 GeV in this scheme); measured 0.937 (163 GeV)
  start at 1e16 GeV      : 0.3 -> 0.737, 0.5 -> 0.983, 1 -> 1.201, 2 -> 1.284, 3 -> 1.302, 5 -> 1.312, 10 -> 1.318
      a large start lands at about 1.318 (top mass 229 GeV in this scheme); measured 0.937 (163 GeV)
  start at Planck        : 0.3 -> 0.793, 0.5 -> 1.016, 1 -> 1.190, 2 -> 1.250, 3 -> 1.263, 5 -> 1.271, 10 -> 1.276
      a large start lands at about 1.276 (top mass 222 GeV in this scheme); measured 0.937 (163 GeV)
  Reading: any large top link at high energy is pulled down toward one value, so the top's link near 1 needs no fine choice. But the pull
  lands 36-41% above the measured top's link in the Standard Model alone (222-229 GeV against 163 GeV in this scheme; the known result of
  Hill 1981). The measured top needs a start of only about 0.41-0.5 at high energy, which is not on the pull. A 'full glass' link of 1
  at high energy lands at about 1.19-1.20 (about 207 GeV): also too heavy.
