B1. Three placements of the same relationship matrix (three seats), instead of three slots of one matrix
====================================================================================================
  Three copies of the 3x3 octonion matrix, one per seat. No number systems are joined, and nothing squares to nothing (a sum of three such matrices).
  All turns: 156. Turns that cannot tell the seats apart (commute with moving every seat one place round): 52 (rank 4).
  With the observer's spot and facing direction: 36 carriers if each seat had its own forces; 12 (rank 4) if the forces are blind to the seat.
  Link states: 3 x 16 = 48 components = three generations of weak pairs. One weak force, the same for all three.
  -> A hole-free arrangement EXISTS: the same shape in three placements (the earlier own wording), with forces that are blind to the placement.
     The seat space carries the 3x3 mass matrices of rule 5; the octonion matrix carries the forces. They are two different matrices.
  Measured: the weak force is the same for all three generations to 0.1-0.2% (tau, muon and pion decays); no force that tells generations apart is seen.

====================================================================================================
B2. What that costs, and what can replace it
====================================================================================================
  'At most 3 generations because octonion matrices stop at 3x3' used the octonion matrix's slots as the generations. With seats
  instead of slots, that argument no longer applies: nothing limits the number of seats.
  What still fixes 3:
   - at least 3: an irremovable twist (matter-antimatter difference) needs 3 (Kobayashi & Maskawa 1973; an earlier test). A theorem.
   - at most 3: only rule 6 ('nothing extra'), which holds again in the earlier picture: the minimum the twist needs, and no more. A rule, not a theorem.
  Data: a fourth generation of the same kind is excluded (Higgs production would be about 9 times larger; Z width gives 2.9963 +/- 0.0074 light neutrinos).
  Other published routes to 3 inside one structure, checked against rule 1:
   - doubling the octonion matrix with complex numbers (Boyle 2020): complex x octonions has holes.
   - the next number system, 16 parts (sedenions; Gillard & Gresnigt 2019): has holes (it fails rule 1 by Hurwitz's theorem).
   - the three four-part systems containing the facing direction (Manogue & Dray 1999): that '3' is already the three colours in TRPG.
  -> None passes rule 1. FAIL for 'one structure gives both the forces and exactly three generations'.

C1. The lepton 45 degree rule as a balance of two pushes
====================================================================================================
  relationship weight / self weight = 0.999993. In the tractor picture: at balance the two pushes are EQUAL (Newton's third law). That is the 45 degree rule.
  A published energy function gives it (Koide 1990): energy = (terms in the total) - k x (self weight) x (relationship weight). With the total kept
  (rule 2), the product is largest when the two weights are equal. It works BECAUSE the function treats self and relationship alike. That is put in.
  Wave test: three seats on a ring joined only by their links (no outside anchor: the full glass), with a twist phi through the ring:
   each seat's energy IS half own motion, half links (the virial theorem), but the three frequencies give a ratio of at most Q = 0.500; leptons have 0.667.
  -> The balance reading restates the rule; the simplest wave model with that balance FAILS to give the lepton masses. The 45 degree rule stays measured.

====================================================================================================
C2. The twist and the electron's mass are the same number
====================================================================================================
  With exact 45 degree balance, the lightest member is massless when the twist is exactly pi/12 = 15 degrees = 0.261799.
  Measured twist: 0.222225 (12.733 degrees). The gap, 2.267 degrees, IS the electron's mass: sqrt(m_e)/average = 0.04035; gap in radians = 0.03957.
  -> 'Why is the twist 2/9?' and 'why is the electron so light but not massless?' are one question, not two. It does not answer either.
