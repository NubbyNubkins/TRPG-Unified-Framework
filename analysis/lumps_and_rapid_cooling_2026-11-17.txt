(1) How rapid is the cooling, compared with the thing that is happening? (process rate / cooling rate; big = slow, gentle cooling)
   the surface's resistance switches on (160 GeV)   1.3e+14
   quarks lock into protons (155 MeV)               1.1e+19
   neutrinos let go (1.5 MeV)                       1 (by definition: this is why they let go)
   first nuclei (0.066 MeV)                         1.6e+02
   atoms form (0.26 eV)                             329 before the bottleneck, about 10 with it; falls to 1 as the last electrons are left over
   -> atom formation is the ONE step where the cooling is rapid compared with the process. That is why its timing depends on how lumpy matter is.

(2) Atoms forming is what lets matter go lumpy (pressure drops): smallest lump that can pull itself together
   just before (matter tied to the light)     sound speed 135,574 km/s; smallest lump 5.5e+17 suns
   just after (atoms, light set free)         sound speed 6 km/s; smallest lump 5.9e+04 suns
   -> true, and large (a factor of 1e13), but these lumps come AFTER atoms form, so they cannot change WHEN atoms form.

(3) When could lumps have been made? Test: lumps already there at 4 minutes would spoil the deuterium (D/H goes as density^-1.6)
   mild 0.6/1/1.4       deuterium x1.28   (measured to 1%: Cooke, Pettini, Steidel 2018)
   medium 0.3/1/1.7     deuterium x2.76   (measured to 1%: Cooke, Pettini, Steidel 2018)
   strong 0.1/1/1.9     deuterium x13.72   (measured to 1%: Cooke, Pettini, Steidel 2018)
   -> lumps must be made AFTER the first nuclei (4 minutes) and BEFORE atoms (363 thousand years): light between 0.07 MeV and 0.3 eV.
