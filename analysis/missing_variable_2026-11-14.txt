0. the equilibrium picture inside the shared-state rule
========================================================================================================
  The shared all-ones part alone (exact equilibrium) has sizes [-0. -0.  3.]: one family takes everything, two are exactly zero.
  Masses of the lighter two families exist ONLY because the matrix sits slightly off that equilibrium (the break). So 'the equilibrium point is
  rarely zero, so there is always a small mass' is exactly this rule's structure. HOLDS as a reading (it is why the hierarchy is steep).
  Size of the offset needed: down ladder ~0.019, up ladder ~0.0039 of the shared part (strange/bottom, charm/top).

Is 'about 2.5' a fixed number?
========================================================================================================
  The shortfall of the turn between families 2 and 3 in this rule: 0.0151 to 0.0162 against 0.0418, i.e. 2.6x to 2.8x depending on sign choices,
  break form and energy. It is the CEILING of the shared-state rule, not a constant of nature. No clean number (2.5, e, 8/3) is singled out.

Do the three tiers give the three generations?
========================================================================================================
  Tier 1 (quarks with gluons) is built from family 1 (up, down); tier 2 (protons with electrons) is family 1 again; tier 3 is the top (family 3).
  Family 2 has no tier. So tiers and families are not the same three. FAIL as a derivation. The three still comes from the 3x3 matrix itself
  (an earlier test: the matrix has exactly three sizes; and here: its shared part leaves exactly one heavy and two light). Hint, not derivation.
