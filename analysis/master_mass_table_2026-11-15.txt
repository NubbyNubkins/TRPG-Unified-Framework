  Home mass = Higgs part (current mass) + equilibrium part (what the strong containment adds):
   up/down  home   363.1 MeV = Higgs     3.4 + equilibrium  359.7 MeV
   strange  home   537.5 MeV = Higgs    93.5 + equilibrium  444.0 MeV
   charm    home  1708.2 MeV = Higgs  1273.0 + equilibrium  435.2 MeV
   bottom   home  5041.4 MeV = Higgs  4183.0 + equilibrium  858.4 MeV
  The equilibrium part is a few hundred MeV for every quark: the same containment energy, roughly, whatever the quark (charm and bottom
  depend on which mass definition is used: with the bottom's pole mass, 4.78 GeV, its equilibrium part is about 260 MeV).
  The proton itself, split by lattice calculations (Yang et al. 2018): quark masses (the Higgs layer) about 9%, quark motion 32%, gluon
  field 37%, the scale (trace) part 23%. So about 91% of the proton is equilibrium energy, as the record's containment principle says.
