All six areas equal J/2 = 1.558e-05 (Jarlskog 1985). Some faces are long and thin, one is nearly equilateral (the one measured at B factories).
The joining volume: det[up ladder, down ladder] = -3.313e+39 i (MeV^12); formula 2 J x (mass-squared gaps) = -3.313e+39: equal.
  So the ladders and the turn form one perspective-free object: a determinant (a volume) whose faces are the six triangles.
Closure (nothing escapes) holds by construction in the 3-family fit (1.000000). Measured directly (PDG 2024): |Vud|^2+|Vus|^2+|Vub|^2 =
  0.99836 +- 0.00073: short of 1 by 0.00164 (2.2 sigma), the 'Cabibbo angle anomaly'. A heavy down-type partner mixing with
  the down quark at an angle of about 0.040 would account for it (Belfatto, Beradze & Berezhiani 2020; Branco et al. 2021).

One partner for both the fold and the leak? (rough estimate, simplest form)
  A heavy partner D coupled with strength g along u gives a fold k*p0 = g^2/M_D and a mixing angle theta = g/M_D, so M_D = k*p0/theta^2.
  V3 (down squeezed, up stretched): fold 0.0660, leak 0.00164 -> M_D ~ 6.4 GeV
  V1 (down only): fold 0.1162, leak 0.00164 -> M_D ~ 11.3 GeV
  The LHC excludes heavy down-type partners below about 1.5 TeV (ATLAS and CMS searches for vector-like quarks). A partner at 6-11 GeV
  is ruled out, so the fold amount and the first-row leak cannot both come from one partner in this simplest form.
