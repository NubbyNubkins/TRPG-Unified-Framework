A1. How many 'i' does the three-way matrix have? (An 'i' = a way to turn every state a quarter turn that all the turns respect)
====================================================================================================
  Matrices that commute with all 46 turns of the three-way product: 2 (the number 1 and one 'i'). With the 45: 2.
  That one 'i' squared = -1 (error 3e-12). So the whole three-way structure has exactly ONE i, up to its sign.
  Matrices that commute with only the 12 force carriers: 14. With the 12 alone, each of the six kinds of state could be given its own
     i, with its own sign: each kind could be made left or right separately. The whole structure does not allow that: one sign for all.
  -> There are two choices and only two: i or -i. That is the observer's facing direction, forward or backward.

     with itself. That one exception is the standard reason the hidden partners are very heavy (Minkowski 1977; Yanagida 1979).

A4. So can 'handedness' be removed and replaced by the perspective? The reasoning, step by step
====================================================================================================
  1. Each of the 16 states is a spinning wave. Relativity's simplest spinning wave has ONE screw sense and is written with an i (Weyl 1929).
  2. Quantum mechanics uses one i for everything (Stueckelberg 1960; tested by Renou et al. 2021). An inherited rule, like relativity.
  3. The three-way matrix has exactly one i for all its states together, the observer's facing direction (A1).
  4. Same i for the wave and for the states: then all 16 states get the same screw sense relative to their charges. No state can be
     flipped separately (A1: one sign for all), and nothing is doubled.
  5. Facing the other way (i -> -i) then changes three names at once: matter <-> antimatter, left <-> right, i <-> -i. These are not three
     facts. And which sense is called 'left' is only a name: a world with every sense reversed is ours seen in a mirror.
  6. What is physical, and measured (Lee & Yang 1956; Wu 1957): the world is not its own mirror image. That follows from A2.
  7. ATTACK: give the wave its own separate i. Then every state comes in both screw senses: mirror states. A state and its mirror can
     always pair off with no Higgs, so the measured masses would have no reason; and rule 6 (nothing extra) forbids the doubling.
     ATTACK: use the 12 carriers alone, without the whole three-way structure. Then each kind of state could be flipped separately (A1: 14
     commuting matrices); most such choices fail the consistency sums. The whole structure allows no such choice.
  -> HOLDS. Nothing the main line has uses 'left' or 'right' as a separate idea: the 12 carriers, the charges, the consistency sums, the
     mass links, pion decay (37.6), neutrino masses and the matter excess all go through unchanged. The earlier added reading 'spin uses
     the same i as the states' is not removed: it becomes part of rule 4, 'one perspective, one i'. One reading fewer, same content.
     What stays inherited, not derived: that matter is made of spinning waves at all (step 1) and that there is one i (step 2).

B1. Gravity's strength as a number
====================================================================================================
  Gravity has no strength 'by itself': G carries units. As a pure number it is a ratio. With the proton as the unit (the 'proton = 1'):
     grain energy (Planck) = 1.2209e+19 GeV = 1.301e+19 protons;  gravity between two protons, as a pure number: 5.906e-39 (the electric force: 1/137).
     The honest size of the question is the logarithm: ln(grain / proton) = 44.01.
  In TRPG : entanglement across a surface grows with its area (Bombelli et al. 1986; Srednicki 1993), and that gives
     Einstein's equations with G = 1/(4 x entanglement per area) (Jacobson 1995). So gravity's strength IS the fineness of the links of space.

B2. Is gravity's strength a separate number from the force strengths? (Two-loop running, as in an earlier test)
====================================================================================================
  Strong = weak at 10^16.46 GeV, where 1/strength = 46.1 (hypercharge there: 37.0).
  At the grain energy: 1/strength = 33.0 (hypercharge), 49.1 (weak), 52.9 (strong).
  The strong force grows slowly from there down to the proton: one-loop count of the logarithm = 2 pi/(7 x strength) = 47.5,
     against ln(grain/proton) = 44.0 (the difference is the heavy-quark steps and the proton's size in units of the strong scale).
  -> Known physics (Gross & Wilczek 1973; Politzer 1973; Wilczek 2001): the proton is light against the grain BECAUSE the strong force is
     weak at the grain (about 1/53) and strengthens only logarithmically. So gravity's strength and the force strengths are TIED: gravity's
     weakness is the same fact as the strong force's weakness at the grain of space. This is the proposed tie. It trades one number
     for another; the count of measured numbers does not drop, and nothing is derived yet.
  TEST: 'gravity is as strong as the other forces where they meet each other'. Gravity's number at energy E is (E/grain)^2. At the meeting
     energy it is 5.7e-06, against 0.0217 for the others: 3,806 times too weak. Put the other way: this predicts a grain
     energy of 10^17.30 GeV against the measured 10^19.09. FAIL as a number (62x in energy), though on the
     logarithmic scale it lands at 91% of the way from the proton to the grain. (Same known gap as Kaplunovsky 1988.)
     Gravity actually reaches the others' strength at about 10^18.24 GeV, 60 times above the meeting energy.
  TEST: 'gravity's strength from counting TRPG's own states' (Sakharov 1967; Dvali 2007). States: 3 x 16 matter x 2 + 12 carriers x 2 + 4 Higgs = 124.
     Grain = Planck/sqrt(N) = 10^18.04 GeV to Planck x sqrt(12 pi/N) = 10^18.83 GeV, depending on how the cut is made.
     The count turns a grain SIZE into a strength; it does not give the size. No TRPG scale sits there. FAIL as a derivation.

B3. Gravity and the Higgs field, in both directions
====================================================================================================
  Direction 1, 'the Higgs field sets gravity's strength' (Zee 1979): needs a Higgs-to-curvature link of 9.8e+31.
     Measured limit from Higgs rates at the LHC: below 2.6e15 (Atkins & Calmet 2013). FAIL by a factor 4e+16.
     With an ordinary link (1/6), the Higgs field changes gravity's strength by 2e-33 of itself. Nothing.
  Direction 2, 'gravity's grain sets the Higgs' (Shaposhnikov & Wetterich 2010, who predicted 126 GeV two years before the discovery):
     the Higgs's hold must fade to zero exactly at the grain. With today's top mass that needs a Higgs of 128.3 +/- 1.2 GeV (Buttazzo et al. 2013);
     measured 125.2. Off by 2.5%, 2.6 sigma (the same test as an earlier test, B4). A near miss, strict FAIL. The hold fades near
     10^10.5 GeV, the region of TRPG's hidden neutrino partners (about 10^9 to 10^11 GeV), not the grain. That stays a lead.
  The Higgs and dark energy: the Higgs balance stores (104 GeV)^4. Dark energy is (2.25 meV)^4. Ratio 4.6e+54.
     If stored balance energy acted as dark energy the universe would not exist as it is (Weinberg 1989). In gravity-from-entanglement it does
     not: there the dark energy is a constant fixed once, not the energy of any field (Jacobson 1995; Einstein 1919). In TRPG words: it is a
     kept amount of the bag (rule 2), set at the pop. HOLDS as the form (and it is required, or the Higgs would wreck gravity). No value.
     Prediction of this form: dark energy never changes (w = -1 exactly). DESI 2025 hints at a change (2.8 to 4.2 sigma). Live test.
