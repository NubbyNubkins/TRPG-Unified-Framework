## Abstract

We present Dynamic Relational Transitioning Geometry (TRPG), a framework in which the universe is described by the relationships between its parts rather than by the parts alone. It rests on three ideas, one rule and one bet. The ideas: space is one full medium of relationships, seen one perspective at a time, with entanglement first; every bound structure is a balance of outward push against inward pull (containment); and within the lepton families a particle's tie to itself balances its ties to the others. The rule is nothing extra. The bet is that the far side of the relationship matrix is real. Section 2 unpacks these into the seven working principles.

Combined with established physics, these principles reproduce the following.

-   Einstein's gravity, through the entanglement area law and Jacobson's thermodynamic argument. This includes the full deflection of starlight (1.75″) and the Shapiro delay.
-   The expansion of a flat universe, with an age of 13.79 billion years.
-   A containment pressure B = 60 (57 to 64) MeV/fm³, obtained from the proton and Δ masses alone.
-   Carried to neutron stars, that same pressure gives maximum masses of 2.0 to 2.15 solar masses and a tidal deformability Λ₁.₄ = 440 to 580, consistent with observation, provided quarks in the core pair with a gap of about 82 to 97 MeV. That gap is the prediction.
-   The three charged-lepton masses as the eigenvalues of a single relational matrix with one scale.
-   The strong force SU(3) with three colours, once the medium's inner space is required to have no gaps and to conserve the size of relationships; and, reading the forces from the relationship matrix seen from one perspective, with stated readings, which other forces exist and, reading the matrix's states three at a time, every particle of a generation with its measured charge and one-handed matter. The force strengths are taken from measurement.
-   Fractional quark charges, which anomaly cancellation fixes once the number of colours is given.

The framework makes predictions that can fail and that experiments can test. The first is normal neutrino mass ordering (given today's limit from cosmology), with a lightest mass of 0.36 meV and a sum of 0.0592 to 0.0594 eV. The second is a quark pairing gap of about 82 to 97 MeV in neutron-star cores. A third is a strong coupling of 0.1178, on which the meeting of the force strengths rests and which awaits measurement. A fourth, beyond the reach of present and planned experiments, is that neutrinos are their own antiparticles, with neutrinoless double beta decay at an effective mass of 1.3 to 3.9 meV. Tested strictly, two quark-mass relations in the particle matrices pass: the down quark through the Cabibbo angle and the up quark through the decay η → 3π, without lattice input. The force strengths together with gravity's strength, the top, bottom, charm and strange masses with quark mixing, the lepton twist (0.222222, close to 2/9 but measured, and the same open number as the electron's mass) remain open, as do the mass and amount of dark matter (the matrix allows one candidate, a chargeless wave, the majoron, as a lead) and the origin of the seeds of galaxies, whose size and tilt are measured. The matter–antimatter excess is not predicted; it is allowed. Where a result was already established by others (the W and Z mass relation, atomic binding, lepton lifetimes, the Cabibbo angle, hadron and nuclear masses), the framework reaches it by a second route, and each such check is reported with its status: confirmed, fitted, or failed.

## 1. Introduction

Physics has several very successful descriptions of nature. General relativity describes gravity as geometry. Quantum mechanics describes matter as waves of probability. Quantum field theory describes the forces. Cosmology describes the history of the whole. Each is confirmed to remarkable precision, and yet they are usually presented as separate subjects.

This work starts from a different question. What if the great physicists were each correct, and each was looking at the same universe from a different side? Then the task is not to replace any of them. It is to find the single picture in which each of their results is one view.

The picture proposed here is relational. A particle is real on its own, but what it does, and what the universe does, comes from how particles relate to one another. Write down how every particle relates to every other and you obtain a matrix. The structure of physics is the structure of such matrices.

**Purpose and method.** This work starts from existing conclusions. Any idea that disagreed with determined measurements was discarded, and the record of each one is kept. Its purpose is to show that the Standard Model, gravity and quantum mechanics already fit together, building on the work of those who found them. Where a choice was made with today's data in view it is labelled as fitted, not predicted. Its predictions are only for measurements that have not yet been made.

Section 2 states the principles. Section 3 writes them as a single action. Sections 4 to 8 show what follows, each compared with measurement. Section 9 gives the predictions, Section 10 the open questions, and Section 11 concludes.

## 2. Principles

Stated most simply, the framework is three ideas, one rule and one bet. **The ideas.** (1) Space is one full medium of relationships, seen one perspective at a time, with entanglement first. (2) Every structure is a balance of outward push and inward pull (containment). (3) Within the lepton families, a particle's tie to itself and its ties to the others are balanced (45°); for leptons this follows from the family symmetry together with maximum uncertainty between self and relationship (Section 10), and it does not hold for quarks (Section 7). **The rule.** Nothing extra: no proton decay, no fourth generation, no new forces, no mirror particles, and one Higgs pair. **The bet.** The far side of the relationship matrix is real; it gives the strong coupling and the Higgs-mass rule of Section 9, and if it fails the ideas and the rule still stand. Which forces exist follows from the perspective principle by a theorem (Section 8). A whole generation's charges and one-handed matter are reproduced with one further reading of the matrix, which is stated and not derived. The full medium is consistent with the measured flat universe but does not by itself require it. The principles below unpack the three ideas.

1.  **One medium.** Space is like liquid filling a sealed bag. There is no gap between the liquid and anything in it, because the liquid is space itself.
2.  **Always full.** The proportions of matter, radiation and dark energy change, but together they always fill the whole. Energy is conserved, though conservation can be checked only locally.
3.  **Resistance with direction.** Space has a baseline resistance. Mass and energy raise it, equally for time and for space. Waves are energy spreading through regions of different resistance.
4.  **Containment.** Every bound structure is a balance of outward push against inward pull. If the push wins, the structure leaks, as a star does. If the pull wins, it holds everything in, as a black hole does. Inside a proton, quarks push out and gluons pull in. The rule is the same at every size, while the forces differ.
5.  **Relational scale.** Each particle sets its own scale of view, and no scale is privileged by the laws themselves. The proton sits at the scale of the spectrum we physically observe, so it sets our view. The only scale-fair distance between two masses is S = ½ ln(m₁/m₂).
6.  **Entanglement first.** Entanglement is the defining feature of quantum physics, as Schrödinger said in 1935. Entangled partners are joined through the geometry, so nothing needs to cross the distance between them. Gravity follows from entanglement, and uncertainty means that we see one perspective of a single geometry at a time.
7.  **Transitioning geometry.** Space behaves like crossing waves. Locally it curves as peaks, saddles and troughs, and the pattern keeps changing, but its total curvature averages to zero.

## 4. Gravity

**Resistance and the bending of light.** Give space a resistance n = 1 + 2GM/(rc²) around a mass M. Light passing the Sun is then deflected by 1.75″, as measured. The radar echo from planets is delayed by exactly the Shapiro amount. The factor of two matters. A resistance acting on time alone would give half the deflection (0.876″), and one with no direction would give none. The resistance must act on time and space equally. This is the optical form of weak-field general relativity (Eddington, Gordon). Feynman, Weinberg and Deser showed that a long-range field which couples to everything, and which has this tensor character, must be Einstein's gravity.

**Gravity from entanglement.** In a filled medium, the entanglement between a region and its surroundings grows with the area of the boundary rather than with the volume inside. We confirmed this on a lattice of coupled oscillators: a boundary law fits the computed entanglement far better than a volume law. Jacobson (1995) showed that if this area law holds and the relation δQ = T dS holds at every local horizon, Einstein's equations follow. Gravity is then a consequence of entanglement, not an extra ingredient. Related work by Ryu and Takayanagi, and by Van Raamsdonk, connects entanglement directly to the shape of space.

**Gravity's strength.** Entanglement fixes the form of gravity (Bekenstein, 1973; Hawking, 1975; Jacobson, 1995) but not its strength, which is the fineness of the links of space: ln(E~grain~/m~p~) = 44.0. It is tied to the force strengths, since the proton is light compared with the grain because the strong force is weak there and strengthens only slowly (Gross and Wilczek, 1973; Politzer, 1973; Wilczek, 2001); the simple count 2π/(7α~s~) at the grain gives 47.5. The tie trades one measured number for another. Setting gravity equal to the other forces where they meet predicts a grain energy 62 times too low (Kaplunovsky, 1988), and counting the framework's 124 states (Sakharov, 1967) does not give the size. The Higgs field cannot set gravity's strength (Zee, 1979; the required curvature coupling exceeds the LHC limit of Atkins and Calmet, 2013, by 4 × 10¹⁶); conversely, a Higgs self-coupling vanishing at the grain (Shaposhnikov and Wetterich, 2010) needs m~H~ = 128.3 ± 1.2 GeV against 125.2 (Buttazzo et al., 2013), a strict fail at 2.6σ.

**Black holes.** For the remnant of the first observed merger, GW150914, the ringdown is 250 Hz with a damping time of 4.0 ms. This agrees with the Kerr solution and with the measurement. A black hole is fully contained, so no echoes are expected, and none have been seen.

## 5. Cosmology

**The full bag.** If the contents always fill the medium, the density parameters sum to one: Ω~m~ + Ω~r~ + Ω~Λ~ = 1. The universe is spatially flat. Solving the Friedmann equation with Planck 2018 parameters gives an age of 13.79 billion years (measured 13.787 ± 0.020).

**No centre.** The Big Bang is the release of an over-contained singularity. It is not an explosion into empty space: the same amount is everywhere, spreading as waves. Because there is no empty space outside the bag, the "centre" is a moment in time, not a place. Every observer sees the same background glow.

**Flat on average, curved locally.** The simplest crossing-wave surface, z = cos x + cos y, has one peak, two saddles and one trough per cell. By the Gauss--Bonnet theorem its total curvature is exactly zero. Local geometry keeps changing while the whole stays flat, which is what Planck observes.

**Dark energy and dark matter.** With gravity from entanglement, stored field energy does not gravitate as vacuum energy, and the cosmological constant is a constant of integration fixed once (Einstein, 1919; Jacobson, 1995); otherwise the Higgs balance energy, 5 × 10⁵⁴ times the observed value, would dominate (Weinberg, 1989). On the main line the size of dark energy is set by the two ends of space together, the grain and the horizon: √(grain × horizon) = 3.5 meV against 2.24 meV measured, the relation of Cohen, Kaplan and Nelson (1999). This agreement in size follows from the Friedmann equation and is not counted as a derivation; the amount stays one measured number. On this reading dark energy need not be constant, and no law for its change is derived: the holographic formula of Li (2004) gives today's value but changes in the direction opposite to the hint reported by DESI (2025), and is not used. A fixed amount that never changes is kept on record as the alternative. The closeness of the scale to the neutrino masses is noted and not used. No state of the matrix itself is dark matter: its only chargeless state, the hidden neutrino partner, decays in 10⁻¹⁵ s or less, and dark matter as an entanglement response (Verlinde, 2017) matches the galactic acceleration scale (McGaugh, Lelli and Schombert, 2016) but fails published tests of galaxies, the Solar System and clusters. Reading dark matter as the medium's baseline resistance holds for galaxies: cH₀/2π = 1.04 × 10⁻¹⁰ m/s² against Milgrom's scale of about 1.2 × 10⁻¹⁰ m/s² (Milgrom, 1983), and voids push (Hoffman, Pomarède, Tully and Courtois, 2017); it fails for the Bullet Cluster (Clowe et al., 2006) and the CMB (Planck Collaboration, 2020). The real part is the majoron of Section 8, a cold, chargeless wave.

## 6. Containment: the proton and neutron stars

**The proton.** Model the proton and the Δ as bags in containment balance. The pressure B pulls in, and the motion of the quarks pushes out. The pion cloud around the bag is its leakage (the cloudy bag model). We fitted only the measured masses of the proton (938.92 MeV, isospin average) and the Δ (1232 MeV), using the centre-of-mass correction of Donoghue and Johnson and static pion couplings. This gives B = 60.1 MeV/fm³, or 57.1 to 63.7 for a ±5% pion--nucleon coupling. The proton's quark core then has an rms radius of 0.725 fm. As a check, the same calculation with the 1975 settings returns B = 58.4 MeV/fm³, against the published value of about 57.5.

**Neutron stars.** In a neutron-star core, the same pressure B holds the quarks in, and the measured strong interaction pushes out, with a₄ = 1 − 2α~s~/π and α~s~ run to the relevant scale. We solved the Tolman--Oppenheimer--Volkoff equations with Hinderer's tidal equation.

-   With the strong interaction alone, the heaviest star is 1.54 to 1.80 solar masses. This is below the 2.08 ± 0.07 solar masses measured for PSR J0740+6620, so it fails.
-   Quarks in such cores are expected to pair (colour superconductivity). At the central B = 60.1 MeV/fm³, a pairing gap Δ of about 82 to 97 MeV gives a heaviest star of 2.0 to 2.13 solar masses, a radius of 10.8 to 11.3 km for a 1.4-solar-mass star, and a tidal deformability Λ₁.₄ = 440 to 580.
-   These values meet both the pulsar mass and the GW170817 limit (Λ₁.₄ below about 580). Across the full range of B allowed by the proton (57.1 to 63.7 MeV/fm³), the window shifts to Δ ≈ 75 to 108 MeV, higher B requiring a larger gap.

The containment pressure comes from the proton, and the push comes from the measured strong force. The one remaining quantity, the pairing gap, becomes a prediction (Section 9).

**Stars and black holes.** For stars the measure of containment is the compactness C = GM/(Rc²). The Sun has C ≈ 0.000002, a neutron star about 0.18, the last stable configuration 4/9 (Buchdahl's limit), and a black hole 1/2. A star is under-contained and leaks energy. A black hole is over-contained. The same equations describe every step in between.

## 7. Relational masses

**The relational distance.** For any two masses, the quantity S = ½ ln(m₁/m₂) is the only measure that is unchanged when all masses are multiplied by the same factor. Their combined ratio is (m₁ + m₂)/√(m₁m₂) = 2 cosh S. Applying this to every pair of the known particles gives the particle-to-particle matrix. The antiparticle and decay matrices are built in the same way.

**The charged leptons.** Write the square roots of the three charged-lepton masses as the eigenvalues of one circulant matrix, M₀ · circulant(1, re^iδ^, re^−iδ^). With r = 1/√2 and δ = 2/9, the matrix gives the electron, muon and tau masses from a single scale, to 0.003%. The fitted twist is 0.222239 ± 0.000040, against 2/9 = 0.222222. The relation r = 1/√2 is equivalent to Koide's formula (Koide, 1983), in the geometric form of Foot and the parametrisation of Brannen. In the relational reading, the shared part and the relational part of the mass vector are equal, which is exactly r = 1/√2. Each part is 941.6 MeV, within 0.35% of the proton mass. We note this coincidence without claiming an explanation.

**Quarks.** Running all six quark masses to a common scale gives balance angles of 52.15° for the up-type quarks and 48.06° for the down-type, against 45° for the leptons. These angles do not depend on the scale chosen, so they are the targets any theory of quark masses must meet. A simple pattern of link strengths (3/6, 4/6, 5/6 for leptons, down-type and up-type) misses the data by about seven standard deviations and is rejected. The relation √(m~d~/m~s~) ≈ sin θ~C~ (Gatto, Sartori and Tonin, 1968) holds to 0.04%.

**Quark-mass relations, tested strictly.** Each relation contained in the particle matrices was tested with measured masses on both sides and judged against the measurement's own error bar. A full Higgs coupling of the top quark (y~t~ = 1; Hill, 1981) gives m~t~ = 174.1 GeV against 172.57 ± 0.29 GeV (+0.9%, but 5.3σ). The 45° balance applied to the triples (c, b, t) and (s, c, b) (Rivero, 2011) gives m~c~ = 1353 MeV against 1273.0 ± 4.6 MeV (+14.6σ) and m~s~ = 84.5 MeV against 93.5 ± 0.8 MeV (−9.9σ); no standard definition of the charm mass rescues it. An earlier chain that started from the bottom mass alone fed its own high charm value into the strange step and so appeared to work to about 1%. The Cabibbo relation gives m~s~/m~d~ = 19.9 against 20.4 ± 0.3 (−1.6σ), which passes. The up quark is reached through the decay η → 3π, in which electromagnetic effects nearly cancel (Sutherland, 1966; Bell and Sutherland, 1968). Leutwyler's relation (m~u~/m~d~)² + (m~s~/m~d~)²/Q² = 1 (Leutwyler, 1996), with the Cabibbo value of m~s~/m~d~ and Q = 22.1 ± 0.7 from the measured decays (Colangelo, Lanz, Leutwyler and Passemar, 2018), gives m~u~/m~d~ = 0.437 and m~u~ = 2.03 MeV (range 1.72 to 2.28 MeV; measured 2.16 ± 0.07 MeV), with no lattice input. Each relation is published separately; linking them through the matrices is the contribution here. The 45° relations among the heavier quarks are numerical patterns good to 1–10%, not laws, so the bottom, charm and strange masses remain measured inputs.

**Cone or spiral.** Written as exact statements and tested at μ = 162.5 GeV, the lepton ladder is a cone, √m~k~ ∝ 1 + √2 cos(δ + 2πk/3) with opening 45.000° (Koide, 1983; Foot, 1994; Brannen, 2006). The quark openings are 48.150° ± 0.015° (down-type) and 52.147° ± 0.018° (up-type); they differ from 45° by 213σ and 405σ and from each other by 174σ, and a single 45° cone for all six quarks (Kartavtsev, 2011) fails by 13σ. Two cones about a common axis would give zero mixing, so the CKM tilt measures the misalignment of the two cone axes. The spiral (geometric steps) fails in all five forms tested, including Froggatt–Nielsen powers of the Cabibbo parameter (Froggatt and Nielsen, 1979), whose fitted exponents (2.0, 2.7, 4.2, 3.7) are near but not at integers; the quarks are nevertheless much closer to equal logarithmic steps than the leptons. The down-type opening is scale-independent under QCD and remains a fixed value to explain. No quark mass moves.

**Mutual pushes of quarks and leptons.** Renormalization-group running (Gell-Mann and Low, 1954; Machacek and Vaughn, 1984) couples the two sectors in three ways. Through the Higgs, the top shifts every lepton Yukawa coupling by 1.67% per e-fold, the leptons shift the quarks by 6 × 10⁻⁷; this is flavour-universal and changes a ladder's scale, not its shape. Within a weak doublet the top drives the bottom by −0.83% per e-fold with opposite sign, the route by which the two quark ladders bend each other. QED self-running separates the lightest and heaviest lepton by 2.84%, and direct photon exchange between sectors is 10⁻⁵ to 10⁻⁴. None closes a near miss: matching the lepton scale to m~p~/3 requires a common scale of about 1.3 GeV, where the cone opening is 45.05° rather than 45°; quark balance ratios move away from 5/4 and 5/3; and the 6.8% charm and 8.9% strength misses are far larger. The Koide relation holds for pole masses to 10⁻⁵ although QED running should spoil it by 0.35% (Sumino, 2009), so any quark rule must specify its scale before it is tested.

**The three layers of mass.** Every composite mass is a Higgs layer (the elementary masses), a containment layer (constituent masses and hadrons; Sakharov and Zeldovich, 1966; De Rújula, Georgi and Glashow, 1975; 8 light baryons from 3 fitted numbers to 0.56% rms) and a nuclear layer (the semi-empirical binding formula; Weizsäcker, 1935; Bethe and Bacher, 1936; 5 fitted numbers). Together they give the masses of 2,899 nuclides to 0.008% rms (AME2020; Wang et al., 2021). For ⁵⁶Fe the layers are 595 MeV (1.1%), 52,001 MeV (99%) and −494 MeV (−492 MeV measured). The proton is about 9% quark mass, 32% quark kinetic energy, 37% gluon energy and 23% trace anomaly (Ji, 1995; Yang et al., 2018), so about 91% is containment energy. The structure holds; its numbers are fitted, and the bottom layer, the Higgs links, is open. Balance points of particle pairs fall on other particles' masses at chance level.

**Sizes and the proton's home.** The matrix of sizes S~ij~ holds one number per particle. Five patterns in it fail chance tests, including whole powers of one parameter (Froggatt and Nielsen, 1979). Seen from the proton, each particle's balance mass is m~p~/(1 + e^−2S^), a logistic curve (Verhulst, 1838): lighter particles at their own mass, heavier ones capped at the proton, the proton with itself at one half. This is the scale-view principle in exact form and moves no number.

**Scaling backwards.** Run at two loops from the top mass to the Planck energy (Machacek and Vaughn, 1984; Arason et al., 1992), the down-type opening moves from 48.15° to 47.72° and the up-type from 52.15° to 52.31°, while the leptons stay at 45.05°; neither quark ladder is a 45° cone at any energy, and m~b~/m~τ~ reaches only 0.62. The top Yukawa coupling, run down from large values (Pendleton and Ross, 1981; Hill, 1981), lands at 1.28–1.32 against 0.937: right in kind, 36–41% high. A rule for the elementary masses must act on ratios, which do not run.

## 8. Quantum structure

**Entanglement.** Measured entangled pairs show the correlation E(a, b) = −cos(a − b), stronger than any local hidden-variable model allows (Bell). In the relational picture the two particles are one structure seen from two places. This is the content of the ER = EPR proposal of Maldacena and Susskind, and no signal travels faster than light.

**Uncertainty.** Any wave localised to a width Δx has a spread in wavenumber Δk with Δx·Δk ≥ ½. Heisenberg's relation is this property of waves, applied to matter.

**Charges.** For one generation with N colours, the four anomaly-cancellation conditions fix the hypercharges uniquely. The up-quark charge is ½ + 1/(2N) and the down-quark charge is −½ + 1/(2N). For N = 3 these are +2/3 and −1/3. Anomalies do not fix N; it is measured through the neutral-pion decay rate and the ratio of hadron to muon production in electron--positron collisions, and it follows from the inner algebra below.

**Forces, colours and generations.** The measured carrier counts (eight carriers with two neutral, and four with two neutral) allow only SU(3) and SU(2) × U(1) in the classification of Lie algebras (Killing; Cartan), and the global form S(U(3) × U(2)) passes the ℤ~6~ check for every field. A medium with no gaps has an inner algebra without zero divisors, and relationships that keep their size when combined require a conserved norm; by Hurwitz's theorem (1898) only ℝ, ℂ, ℍ and 𝕆 qualify. A force richer than the weak force requires 𝕆, whose symmetry group G~2~ leaves SU(3) when any one direction is fixed, as every observation fixes one; the remaining six directions form three complex ones, which are the three colours. ℍ acting from the left and from the right carries SU(2)~L~ × SU(2)~R~, but only under a different meaning of symmetry from the one that gives colour: on the number systems alone no single meaning yields the strong, weak and hypercharge carriers together, and constructions that join ℂ, ℍ and 𝕆 into one algebra (Dixon, 1994; Furey, 2016) reintroduce zero divisors, which the no-gaps rule forbids. The strong force is therefore derived. Read two at a time, the weak force, hypercharge and left-handedness would be taken from measurement; on the main line, with the relationship matrix read three at a time from one perspective (Section 8), they follow with one stated reading, and only the strengths are measured. The minimal particle content also needs at least one further Higgs field to match the measured masses (Georgi and Jarlskog, 1979). Hermitian octonionic matrices satisfy the Jordan identity only up to 3 × 3 (Jordan, von Neumann and Wigner, 1934), while an irremovable CP-violating phase needs at least three generations (Kobayashi and Maskawa, 1973), so on this picture the number of generations is exactly three. These steps rest on an interpretive identification of the physical picture with algebraic rules. That forces are local symmetries (Weyl; Yang and Mills) follows from relativity and quantum mechanics, which the framework inherits. Completed with the 4 × 4 group of Pati and Salam, this route is kept on record as the alternative picture; the main line is the relationship-matrix picture below.

**Mass as a balance point.** Mass is the energy at which containment and leakage balance. For the proton this gives a mass of four times the containment energy, about 99% of the proton's mass when only the up and down quark masses are counted as Higgs mass; counting the strange quark the Higgs share is about 9%, and with the heavy quarks' indirect effect about 29% (Shifman, Vainshtein and Zakharov, 1978); the rest is set by one Higgs balance point (246.22 GeV), and one containment pressure (B = 60.1 MeV/fm³) serves both the proton and the neutron star. Fission is a shift of the balance (166 MeV against 173.3 MeV measured). The shape of each force follows from what space does to its field: spreading (1/r²), confinement in a tube (constant pull), or screening (short reach); the nuclear force is shared leakage carried by pions. As strict numbers the balance falls short: the pull between quarks is 0.155 against 0.18–0.19 GeV², and the deconfinement temperature 105 against 156.5 ± 1.5 MeV. It does not give elementary-particle masses, the strengths of the forces, or which forces exist.

**The main picture of the forces: the relationship matrix.** Where gravity emerges from entanglement, an exact symmetry must come with a force (Harlow and Ooguri, 2019; proven for spaces with a boundary), so forces are expected rather than added. Treating matter itself as motion in an inner space fails: a charged particle would weigh about 5 × 10¹⁷ GeV, and a smooth inner space gives no left-handed matter (Witten, 1983). The 3 × 3 Hermitian octonionic matrix, read as the relationship matrix seen by an observer who stands at one point and faces one way, has exactly 12 carriers, 4 neutral, in blocks 8 | 3 | 1, with one meaning of symmetry and no zero divisors (Todorov and Dubois-Violette, 2018; Dubois-Violette and Todorov, 2019; Krasnov, 2019; Baez, 2020–21; reproduced here). The weak pairs carry hypercharges in the ratio 3 : 1, and anomaly cancellation then fixes their partners at +2/3, −1/3 and −1. The matrix lacks exactly two of the partners a whole generation needs, those of the up quark and the electron (four states, one octonion seen from one perspective), and these partners and the strengths are one problem. Counting only what the matrix holds, one common strength predicts sin²θ~W~ = 0.75 where the forces meet, against about 0.43 from the measured values; counting the whole generation (Chamseddine and Connes, 1996) gives 0.375, and, run to the Z mass at two loops, 0.2107 against 0.2312, 8.9% off, the near miss of Georgi, Quinn and Weinberg (1974) and still a fail (Georgi and Glashow, 1974; Baez and Huerta, 2010). The strengths therefore remain inputs. Four homes for the missing partners built from two-way combinations (relationships between two links, the matrix's other turns, the doubled link space and a 4 × 4 matrix, which is not a consistent algebra) all fail, and the three-way reading below supplies them; the generations cannot be slots inside the same matrix, so they are taken as three placements of the one shape, leaving “at least three” as a theorem and “at most three” resting on the nothing-extra rule and measurement; and a right-handed force cannot coexist with it, which removes the left–right scale and leaves about 0.4 of the observed matter excess. On this picture handedness is the side the observer faces: with the no-gaps rule applied to spin (a second added reading), mirror-image partners would be a gap, so matter must be one-handed, and anomaly consistency leaves the measured arrangement (Lee and Yang, 1956; Wu, 1957). The lepton 45° balance holds to 15 parts in a million and follows, for leptons only, from the family symmetry and maximum uncertainty (Section 10), and the twist and the electron's mass are one open number. Any new force carrier would exclude it. Which forces exist is not a choice within this picture. Take the perspective principle as one place of the matrix kept and one imaginary direction kept. The symmetry group of the place is Spin(9), acting on the 16 real numbers that link it to the other two, and the part of Spin(9) that respects the direction is exactly 12-dimensional, 11 + 1, where 11 can only be 8 + 3; the result is the same for every direction (computed here for all seven basis directions and a random one). The eight complex link states carry the remaining charge in the ratio 3 : 1 with multiplicities 6 and 2, a quark pair in three colours and a lepton pair (Günaydin and Gürsey, 1973; Todorov and Dubois-Violette, 2018; Krasnov, 2019). The three forces therefore follow from the perspective principle by a theorem. What the theorem does not give are the strengths and the missing partners. This picture is carried as the main line, with the 4 × 4 picture kept on record as the alternative.

**A relationship among three.** The first principle is read at two levels: two at a time for the numbers in the matrix, which forces the octonions, and three at a time for the states built from them, so that nothing combined with its own mirror image and itself gives nothing. The three-way product {x, y, z}, with the middle state entering as its mirror image (Jordan, 1933; Loos, 1977), has no zero divisors of this kind on the matrix's link states; the remaining two-way ones, “the same state twice gives nothing”, are for matter the exclusion principle (Pauli, 1925). With the perspective read as the spot kept exactly and the facing kept as a direction (overall phase left out), the turns of the three-way product that respect it number exactly 12, four neutral, in blocks 8 | 3 | 1, and the 16 link states are a whole generation with the measured charges: quark pair +1/6, lepton pair −1/2, down partner +1/3, up partner −2/3, electron partner +1 and neutral partner 0. All anomaly sums vanish, no mirror states exist, and the two states picked by the perspective are the neutral and electron partners. This is the whole-generation pattern of Georgi (1975) and Fritzsch and Minkowski (1975), without their extra carriers. Other readings give 11, 13 or 14 turns or inconsistent charges, so the reading is an added step; applied to ℂ and ℍ it gives 3 and 5 kept turns, and only 𝕆 gives 8 | 3 | 1. Only 9 of the 12 turns coincide with the two-way count, so the weak force is re-identified: half the states are weak pairs and half singles, as measured, replacing the two-way reading above, under which every state was a pair. The strengths are unchanged: the geometry gives the whole-generation count, 0.375 with strong equal to weak, 8.9% off at the Z mass at two loops. Charges as literal bound sets of three (Harari, 1979; Shupe, 1979) fail; the count of colour switches holds as a pattern (Georgi and Glashow, 1974; Furey, 2016). The framework adopts this reading.

**Perspective in place of handedness.** The three-way structure has exactly one i: only 1 and the facing direction commute with all 46 of its turns, so every state takes its screw sense from it and the 16 states cannot be wound independently. The perspective rule reads: one spot, one facing, one i throughout. Reversing the facing (i → −i) exchanges matter with antimatter and left with right together, and the charge list of the 16 states differs from its mirror image, so the world is not mirror-symmetric (Weyl, 1929; Lee and Yang, 1956; Wu, 1957). A separate i for spin would double every state into mirror pairs, excluded by the nothing-extra rule; the earlier reading that spin uses the same i becomes part of the perspective rule. Mass is the link between a paired and a single state through the far side of the matrix, whose 10 parts are a colourless weak pair of charge ±1/2 (the Higgs field's numbers) and six coloured parts (Freudenthal, 1954); each state has exactly one mass partner, but the four links come out equal, so their sizes are not given, and the coloured parts must be read as matrix entries rather than particles (Dimopoulos and Georgi, 1981). The same link gives no quark mixing, the right starting point, but not the small measured tilt.

**The forces that build the atom.** The largest set of the 12 turns under which the 16 states are their own mirror image is unique: the 8 strong turns plus one mixture of the single turn with a weak turn, which is electric charge, the two mirror-symmetric forces that bind (Vafa and Witten, 1984). Settling the colourless far-side doublet keeps exactly these 9 turns, the other three becoming W^±^ and Z; this is one statement rather than two, since a mass link maps the world onto its mirror image, and that the far side settles, in the direction keeping most turns (Michel and Radicati, 1971), is not derived. The kept charges give proton +1, neutron 0 and hydrogen 0 exactly (measured to 10⁻²¹; Bressi et al., 2011). The state count gives the direction of running: colour confined near the proton scale, the weak force hidden 26 orders of magnitude before it would confine, electric charge free. The Higgs link is a Yukawa force whose charge is mass (Yukawa, 1935), the link that gives mass rather than the third binding force, which is B − L (below), with range 1/534 of the proton radius; it binds nothing and is not gravity, since universality of free fall holds to 1.5 × 10⁻¹⁵ (MICROSCOPE, 2022) while the Higgs charge per unit mass is 1 for the electron and about 0.29 for the proton. A Higgs coupling to all energy would raise the LHC Higgs rate about 130-fold (ATLAS and CMS, 2022). With two settled directions, a complex far side allows a free top-to-bottom ratio (Lee, 1973), but bottom = τ still fails. The whole matrix read three-way has exactly three independent sizes and no larger such matrix exists (Jordan, von Neumann and Wigner, 1934; Albert, 1934), a hint for three generations but not a derivation, since one matrix cannot hold both forces and generations.

**The energy the Higgs does not give, and matter number.** Under the 12 turns exactly one Higgs-free mass term is allowed: the hidden neutrino partner with itself, the state that the perspective rule keeps exactly and that therefore carries no charge. With no perspective, or with the spot held only as a direction, there is none. This is the Majorana mass of the seesaw (Majorana, 1937; Minkowski, 1977; Yanagida, 1979; Gell-Mann, Ramond and Slansky, 1979); its size is an input. Keeping the spot exactly breaks one further generator, a 13th commuting with the 12, whose charges are +1/3 on quarks and −1 on leptons: B − L (Davidson, 1979; Marshak and Mohapatra, 1980). Strong, electric and B − L form the largest facing-blind subset of the 13. The far side settling breaks the weak generators and gives the Dirac masses; the spot breaks B − L and gives the Majorana mass; together they leave the 9 of SU(3) × U(1)~em~. Two readings are possible: (ii) B − L is gauged above about 10¹¹ GeV, with fifth-force limits (Wagner et al., 2012) requiring a heavy carrier; or (i) the spot is kept exactly and B − L is never a force. The main line adopts (i); (ii) is recorded as the alternative. In reading (i) the phase of the field that settles the Majorana mass survives as a massless or nearly massless Goldstone boson, the majoron (Chikashige, Mohapatra and Peccei, 1981), which is part of a piece the framework already has rather than an extra state. The Higgs-free mass does not separate the generations (no such term touches a charged state; a common value gives m₂/m₃ = 0.0035 against 0.172), and a generation-blind Higgs fails the LHC muon-to-tau comparison by about 68σ (ATLAS and CMS, 2022). The Higgs value, G and the cosmological constant are all properties of space rather than turns, but the Higgs value melts above 159.5 GeV (D'Onofrio and Rummukainen, 2016) and trial relations among them miss by factors of 90 to 450. The matrix supplies a ℤ₂, (−1)^3(B−L)^, under which all 16 states are odd and all 10 far-side parts even (Krauss and Wilczek, 1989). Among the 16 it protects only the lightest neutrino, which gives 0.5% of the dark matter (Kadastik, Kannike and Raidal, 2010). For the far-side parts the sign cannot be exact: if it were, the lightest neutral far-side weak state would be stable, and a stable state of that kind is excluded by direct-detection searches by about two orders of magnitude; it would also forbid the mixing with ordinary matter through which the far-side parts decay. The sign is therefore approximate there, and no prediction rests on it. The one dark matter candidate the matrix allows is the majoron, as a lead. With the settling scale near the heaviest partner, its lifetime is 10²⁵ to 10⁴⁸ s; below about 37 eV it behaves as wave dark matter (Hu, Barkana and Gruzinov, 2000; Hui et al., 2017), allowed above about 2 × 10⁻²⁰ eV (Rogers and Peiris, 2021). Its mass and abundance are not derived (Berezinsky and Valle, 1993; Garcia-Cely and Heeck, 2017). Produced by misalignment (Preskill, Wise and Wilczek, 1983; Abbott and Sikivie, 1983; Dine and Fischler, 1983) after a start colder than the heavy partners, it is all of the dark matter when m_J = 8.8 keV × (6.3 × 10¹⁰ GeV ÷ f)⁴ ÷ angle⁴, where f is the settling value and the angle is where the field started, so its mass is not fixed, and it is cold; a hot start leaves about 73% of it hot, which fails. A non-thermal start that hands its energy directly to the heavy partners (Lazarides and Shafi, 1991) avoids this and keeps leptogenesis, at the cost of one number.

**Neutrinos and the matter–antimatter excess.** If the neutrino shares the relation matrix of the charged leptons, the seesaw mechanism (Minkowski; Yanagida; Gell-Mann, Ramond and Slansky; Glashow; Mohapatra and Senjanović) gives heavy partners of 7.3 × 10⁵, 1.3 × 10⁹ and 6.3 × 10¹⁰ GeV. The heaviest needs a right-handed coupling of about 0.11, consistent with left–right breaking at 10^11.7^ GeV, the scale at which the three measured force strengths unify in the minimal Pati–Salam model (proton lifetime about 10³⁶ years). Flavoured leptogenesis from these partners (Fukugita and Yanagida, 1986), solved with the full rate equations (Buchmüller, Di Bari and Plümacher; Blanchet and Di Bari; Nardi, Nir, Roulet and Racker), does not predict the observed baryon asymmetry of 6.1 × 10⁻¹⁰: with the shared-matrix rule read literally and only the unmeasured phases free, the largest value found is about 1.5 × 10⁻¹³, some 4,000 times short. One narrow region survives every test tried and allows the observed value: δ ≈ 274°, consistent with current neutrino data, a hot start within a window of about five in temperature (capped by magnetic monopoles; Zeldovich and Khlopov, 1978; Preskill, 1979), and a lightest partner almost unlinked to the electron. The same shared-matrix rule fails for quark pairs (m~t~/m~b~ = 41), so it is stated for leptons only.

**Lifetimes.** Lepton universality, written relationally, gives the tau lifetime from the muon's as τ~τ~ = τ~μ~ · BR · e^−10S^. This equals 289.7 fs, against 290.3 fs measured.

**Carrier and energy matrices.** Every interaction joins two matter particles through one carrier. Written as matrices, the photon, Z, W, gluon and Higgs couplings reproduce the measured charges, and the main line predicts ρ = m~W~²/(m~Z~² cos²θ~W~) = 1 exactly, because the far side holds exactly one weak doublet of hypercharge ½ (Veltman, 1977); measured, 1.0003 ± 0.0005 after known corrections. Veltman's condition for cancelling quadratic divergences (Veltman, 1981) needs a 313 GeV Higgs, texture zeros (Fritzsch, 1977) miss the mixing, and equal entries miss: the open masses are the Higgs-matrix entries and the tilt is the W matrix. The pair link value (m₁ + m₂)/√(m₁m₂) is exactly the inverse reduced-mass factor, so the binding energies of hydrogen (13.598 eV), positronium (6.803 eV), muonium (13.540 eV) and muonic hydrogen (2.528 keV) are reproduced, as in Bohr's treatment (Bohr, 1913) with the reduced mass; this is a reformulation, not an independent test. The same matrices give the top width (1.35 GeV against 1.42 (+0.19 −0.15) GeV) and light hadron masses to a few per cent with five fitted numbers (De Rújula, Georgi and Glashow, 1975). Gravity written as a perspective fails in matrix form; for every elementary pair the Higgs-to-gravity force ratio is 1.96 × 10³². The Higgs cannot be a top-quark condensate (Bardeen, Hill and Lindner, 1990).

## 9. Predictions

**Awaiting measurement.** Each result in this section follows within the framework but rests on a measurement that has not yet returned. None is counted as established until it does; the deciding measurement is named with each.

**The strong coupling (awaiting measurement).** The force strengths meet at 4.6 × 10¹³ GeV if the three colourless far-side weak pairs of the relationship matrix sit at 2.4 × 10⁸ GeV (Section 10). At three loops this requires α~s~(M~Z~) = 0.1178, with the grain of space fixed in advance as the Planck energy divided by √2. The grain was fixed on 17 November 2026, before the deciding measurement. It, and the form of the rule for the pairs' weight, were chosen with today's data in view (fitted, not predicted), so only later measurements test the prediction; the unreduced Planck energy would have given 0.1196, which is not the framework's value. The world average is 0.1180 ± 0.0009; the most precise single determination is 0.11873 ± 0.00056 (Dalla Brida and others, 2025), which lies 1.7 standard deviations above it and does not yet decide. Lattice determinations at about 0.3% will. An observed proton decay would also falsify this part.

**The Higgs mass (awaiting measurement).** The framework's balance of containment, applied at the grain of space, says that the Higgs holds itself together down to the grain and no further, so its self-coupling vanishes there. This is the rule of Shaposhnikov and Wetterich (2010), which assumes that gravity stays well behaved at the grain (Weinberg, 1979; Reuter, 1998). Run at two loops (Machacek and Vaughn, 1984; Luo, Wang and Xiao, 2003) from the starting values of Buttazzo et al. (2013), the Standard Model's states alone give m~H~ = 128.8 ± 1.5 GeV, which is 2.4 standard deviations above the measured 125.20 ± 0.11 GeV. With the far-side states of the relationship matrix included, three colourless weak pairs at 2.4 × 10⁸ GeV and their three coloured partners at 4.6 × 10¹³ GeV, both fixed beforehand by the force strengths and not adjusted here, the result is 127.2 ± 1.5 GeV, within 1.3 standard deviations. The test and its pass line were fixed before it was run. The Higgs mass therefore leaves the list of measured inputs, while the Higgs value stays measured. This is not counted as established until measurement returns. The rule needs a top pole mass of 171.6 GeV against 172.57 ± 0.29 GeV measured; the quoted error allows 0.5 GeV for the ambiguity in what the measured top mass means, and without that allowance the gap is 1.9 standard deviations; on the top mass alone (171.6 needed against 172.57 ± 0.29 measured) it is 3.2. A sharper top mass or strong coupling that pushes the gap beyond 3 standard deviations would falsify it. Any extra weak matter at high energy would help in the same way, so the agreement does not single out this framework.

**No proton decay.** In the 27 states of each generation (Gürsey, Ramond and Sikivie, 1976; Slansky, 1981) every charge is exact and all five anomaly sums vanish. With the framework's content, the twelve known carriers, one Higgs pair, the majoron and no coloured spin-0 states, no renormalisable interaction changes baryon number, since that takes three quarks at once (Weinberg, 1979b; Wilczek and Zee, 1979). The only remaining source is the grain of space itself, which gives a lifetime near 10⁴⁴ years against the measured floor of 2.4 × 10³⁴ years (Takenaka et al., 2020). The statement rests on the nothing-extra rule: coloured partners of the Higgs would bring the decay back.

**Neutrino masses.** The same relational rule, applied to neutrinos with the two measured mass splittings, gives, with today's limit from cosmology, normal ordering. The masses are m₁ = 0.36 meV, m₂ = 8.7 meV and m₃ = 50.1 meV, summing to 0.0592 eV; with the most recent global fit of the splittings m₃ = 50.3 meV and the sum is 0.0594 eV. The rule also has an inverted solution, with a sum of 0.103 eV; it is excluded by the current cosmological bound, not by the rule itself. The sum lies just above the minimum normal ordering allows (0.0588 eV), so the sharpest number is the lightest mass. JUNO will measure the ordering within a few years, and cosmological surveys are approaching the required sensitivity to the sum. Inverted ordering, or a sum clearly different from 0.059 eV, would falsify this part of the framework.

**Neutrino mixing (conditional).** Exact tribimaximal (Harrison, Perkins and Scott, 2002) and trimaximal mixing are excluded by the data, while the TM1 pattern (Xing and Zhou, 2007; Albright and Rodejohann, 2009) survives within 1σ with sin²θ~12~ = 0.318. If the neutrino flavours are perspectives on one relational structure of this kind, the CP-violating phase lies near 270°, close to maximal CP violation; current global fits weakly prefer 180° to 215°, and DUNE and Hyper-Kamiokande will decide.

**Quark pairing in neutron stars.** With the containment pressure set by the proton, neutron stars require a quark pairing gap of about 82 to 97 MeV (75 to 108 MeV across the proton's range of B). Theoretical estimates span 50 to 150 MeV, and the gap has not been measured. A firm determination outside this band, from lattice or effective-theory calculations, or from neutron-star cooling and merger signals, would falsify this part. The range of 2.0 to 2.15 solar masses is the observed window that the gap must respect, not a separate prediction. A star clearly above about 2.15 solar masses would break this part; PSR J0952−0607, at 2.35 ± 0.17 solar masses (Romani and others, 2022), and the 2.6 solar-mass object in GW190814 (Abbott and others, 2020) are the cases to watch.

**Neutrinoless double beta decay.** The Higgs-free mass of Section 8 exists only if neutrinos are Majorana particles (Majorana, 1937). Neutrinoless double beta decay (Furry, 1939) then occurs with an effective mass of 1.3 to 3.9 meV, below the present limit of 28 to 122 meV (KamLAND-Zen, 2024) and below the 10 to 20 meV reach of planned experiments. Proof that neutrinos are Dirac particles, or an effective mass clearly outside this range, would falsify it.

**Unmeasured values.** The carrier and energy matrices give values not yet measured: the Higgs self-couplings (three-point 191 GeV, four-point 0.776), the HHWW coupling (0.213), Higgs couplings to the charm quark, the electron and the light quarks equal to m/246 GeV, and the binding energies of true muonium (1.407 keV), tauonium (23.66 keV) and the proton–tau atom (16.35 keV). Most are standard physics in the framework's form, so they test its bookkeeping rather than new physics. The dark matter candidate is a cold, chargeless spin-0 wave decaying only to neutrinos (its mass is not fixed); dark matter that feels the weak force would falsify it.

## 10. Open questions

**Quark masses.** Of the relations in Section 7, only the down and up links pass strict tests; the bottom, charm and strange masses are measured inputs. The up-quark step uses no lattice QCD, but its value of Q comes from a dispersion analysis of measured decays, whose ±3% uncertainty dominates. Every relation tried for the bottom mass fails, including b = τ at the left–right scale (26% off) and the 45° colour rule, which misses charm by 15–19σ. The scale-free balance angles of Section 7 remain the targets (the down-type cone opening is 48.15° ± 0.015° with current lattice masses); the cone fits the leptons only, and the spiral fits no ladder exactly. Quark mixing belongs with these masses: the simplest mass link gives none, and the small measured tilt is not produced. The strongest lead is a Schur (Feshbach) fold of one heavy far-side colour-triplet state of charge −1/3 (the D quark of the 27 of E~6~; Gürsey, Ramond and Sikivie, 1976) along the TM1 direction (−1, −1, 2) (Harrison, Perkins and Scott, 2002; Schur, 1917; Feshbach, 1958): with one fitted strength all three mixing angles come within 19%, and 2% of random directions do as well. The direction deepens the ladder's existing break; the 19% excess is far outside the errors, so it is a lead, not a result.

**Force groups (awaiting measurement).** Section 8 obtains the strong force and three colours from the full-medium rules (building on Hurwitz; Günaydin and Gürsey; Dixon; Furey; Connes and Chamseddine). In the main picture, the relationship matrix read three at a time from one perspective holds every particle of a generation with its measured charge, the measured carrier counts, the weak hypercharges and one-handed matter, at the cost of stated readings; the 4 × 4 picture, in which the weak force, hypercharge and handedness had to be taken from measurement, is kept on record as the alternative. Only the force strengths remain under this heading. With the standard two-loop method the Standard Model alone misses the measured weak mixing by −8.9% ± 0.1%. The gap closes if the three colourless far-side weak pairs of the matrix (spin ½, no colour, one per generation) sit at 2.2 × 10⁸ GeV, with the coloured far-side states at the meeting, 4.6 × 10¹³ GeV; the doublet–triplet split is the known one (Dimopoulos and Wilczek, 1981). These states add nothing to the matrix. Their weight is the meeting energy squared divided by the grain of space, 2.4 × 10⁸ GeV, which agrees with the weight the strengths require. Stated exactly, the bet is one assumption: the weak pairs have no direct link to the background value that weighs their coloured partners, only the gravitational one. A single shared E₆ coupling would weigh both alike, put the pairs at the meeting energy and fail to close the strengths; the two couplings are not tied together here because the forces never merge into one group (Masiero, Nanopoulos, Tamvakis and Yanagida, 1982). This is assumed, not derived. They cannot be stable, since any produced during reheating would exceed the dark matter many times over (Chung, Kolb and Riotto, 1999; Giudice, Kolb and Riotto, 2001); they decay through mixing with the ordinary lepton doublets, the colourless counterpart of the mixing that tilts the down quarks. Not derived: the meeting energy itself, the grain factor, and gravity's strength. The test is the strong coupling (Section 9). This criterion stays partly met until that measurement returns.

**The twist.** The measured twist, 0.222222 to about one part in a million, matches 2/9 but is not derived. It cannot be exactly 2/9 if the 45° balance is also exact, since the two together miss the electron-to-muon mass ratio by 10 parts per million; reading 2/9 as a fraction of a ring is not an explanation. Rules that reproduce it for the leptons (δ = Q/3 and related forms) fail for both quark families. 

**The 45° balance, derived for leptons.** Two of the framework's principles give it. Three placements of one shape (Section 8) mean the family matrix of amplitudes, the square roots of the masses, is unchanged by a turn of 120°, which makes it circulant, with a the tie to itself and b the ties to the others (Brannen, 2006). Maximum uncertainty between self and relationship means the self share of the matrix's weight, p = 3a²/(3a² + 6|b|²), is ½; every symmetric measure of uncertainty peaks there (Shannon, 1948). And p = ½ is exactly |b|² = a²/2, which is Koide's Q = 2/3 and the 45° angle (Koide, 1983; Foot, 1994). The charged leptons measure p = 0.50000, and the tau mass follows at 1776.969 MeV, +0.9σ. For the neutrinos p = 0.5004 with the lightest amplitude negative, which is a consistency check only, because their masses were built from the rule. For the quarks p = 0.456 (down-type) and 0.393 (up-type), and no choice of signs reaches ½, so the principle as stated does not hold for them; colour is the named difference and why it changes the balance is open. Maximum uncertainty is itself a starting principle of the framework and is not derived from anything deeper. Scripts: `analysis/derive_45_and_sqrt2_2026-11-18.py`, which also shows that the grain factor √2 is not derived (five equally natural readings give five factors), and `analysis/matrix_reading_from_rule4_2026-11-18.py`.

**The matter–antimatter excess.** Not predicted; allowed in one narrow region (Section 8). With majoron dark matter the hot start that region needs fails, so the excess is carried by non-thermal leptogenesis (Lazarides and Shafi, 1991), with one number; the field that settles the Majorana mass cannot itself be that start.

**Gravity's strength and dark matter.** Gravity's strength and the three force strengths are one open problem, the strengths at the grain of space (Section 4). Dark matter is read as partly perspective (Section 5) and partly the majoron (Section 8); its mass and amount are open. A gravitational breaking of the kept spot does not fix the mass: counted correctly it gives about 1% of the dark matter at the settling value used here, and some power of the breaking lands for almost any settling value, so it is not evidence. The majoron as dark matter makes no directly observable number; it is a candidate that fits every limit, not a tested result.

**The Higgs side, the generations and the proton's mass.** Why the far side settles, and at 246 GeV, is not derived; nor is how the Higgs link distinguishes the three placements, which is the single question behind the heavy quark masses, the mixing tilt and the twist. Whether the entanglement that gives gravity and the colour balance that gives the proton's mass are one relationship is open.

**The seeds of galaxies and quantum probabilities.** The size and tilt of the first ripples from which galaxies grew are measured and not derived; what laid them down is open. The framework inherits the probabilities of quantum mechanics (Born, 1926) and does not derive them.

**The Sun's centre.** Containment allows a dense central object that leaks, with fusion continuing in the gas around it. That gas is gas at our scale, but at the scale of the central object it is particles too, and keeping these scales apart is essential. Helioseismology near the core is the test.

**Summary of status.** Derived from the rules: the octonions and the strong force with three colours; which other forces exist (by theorem from the perspective principle); for leptons, the 45° balance; with stated readings, every particle of a generation (16 states, the up and electron partners included) with its measured charge, one-handed matter with no mirror states and one i throughout, which mass links exist, and the same forces for all generations; at least three generations; the form of gravity; mass as balance energy. Measured rules, not derived: the twist, and the quark balance angles. Passing strict tests: the down and up quarks, the anomaly sums, the neutrino mass total, neutron-star masses and fission. Taken from measurement: the three force strengths, the Higgs balance point, the lepton scale and twist, the top, bottom, charm and strange masses, quark mixing, the proton's two containment numbers, the strength of gravity and the amount of dark energy; strictly counted, one lepton number and one quark number fewer than the Standard Model, with the neutrino masses tied to the leptons. Open, each needing a new idea: the strengths at the grain of space (the force strengths, 8.9% off at the Z mass for the Standard Model alone and closed by the far-side weak pairs if the strong coupling is 0.1178, which awaits measurement, together with gravity's strength); why the Higgs side settles, and at 246 GeV; how the Higgs link distinguishes the three placements, which holds the heavy quark masses, the mixing tilt and the twist (2/9, the same number as the electron's mass); why three generations and no more (the matrix's three sizes are a hint only); the matter excess, allowed with one number; dark matter's mass and amount, with the majoron as the candidate; and whether gravity's entanglement and the proton's balance are one relationship. Joining parts the theory already has brings the strengths, the heavy quarks with the tilt, the matter excess and dark matter each down to one missing rule for one number; a free number set to make a match is not a derivation, so none of them passes.

**Established results reached by a second route.** Confirmed exactly, with no measured number: which forces exist, the charges of a generation, neutral atoms and a colour-neutral proton, and ρ = 1 (Glashow, 1961; Weinberg, 1967; Salam, 1968; Veltman, 1977). Confirmed within measurement: atomic bindings (Bohr, 1913, in relational form), the tau lifetime (Fermi, 1934), the top width, the tau mass (Koide, 1983), the pion decay ratio (Marciano and Sirlin, 1993), the Cabibbo angle (Gatto, Sartori and Tonin, 1968), the down and up quarks, the heaviest neutron stars, and the proton's mass as mostly binding energy (Ji, 1995; Yang et al., 2018). Reproduced with fitted numbers, as in the standard treatments: light baryons and nuclear binding. Holding in part: the galactic acceleration scale (Milgrom, 1983) and the top fixed point (Pendleton and Ross, 1981; Hill, 1981). Failing: the common strength of the forces (Georgi, Quinn and Weinberg, 1974) and the Higgs mass from asymptotic safety (Shaposhnikov and Wetterich, 2010).

## 11. Conclusion

A single relational picture, in which space is a full medium, structures are set by containment, and entanglement comes first, reproduces gravity, cosmic expansion, the proton's containment pressure, the masses of the heaviest neutron stars and the charged-lepton masses, and, through two quark-mass relations, the down and up quarks. Required to have no gaps, its inner space yields the strong force with three colours, and its relationship matrix, seen from one perspective, gives which other forces exist, their charges and one-handed matter, each with a stated reading. It does this with about 28 measured numbers, against about 34 for the Standard Model with gravity and cosmology counted the same way. It makes predictions that experiments will test, among them the neutrino mass ordering and scale, the quark pairing gap and the strong coupling, and one, neutrinoless double beta decay at 1.3 to 3.9 meV, that lies beyond present reach. Read three at a time, its states are every particle of a generation with the measured charges. Its binding forces are exactly those blind to the observer's facing, giving a colour-neutral proton and a neutral atom. Where others established a result first, from the W and Z mass relation to atomic binding and nuclear masses, it reaches the same numbers by a second route, and says plainly where that route is fitted or fails. Dark matter's amount is measured; it is read as partly perspective, and the matrix allows one candidate for the rest, a cold, chargeless wave, the majoron, as a lead. The force strengths with gravity's, the settling of the Higgs side, the heavy quark masses and quark mixing, the lepton twist, the upper limit of three generations, the seeds of galaxies and what dark matter is remain open, as they are for all of physics, and the matter excess is allowed but not predicted. The framework does not ask any established result to be given up. It asks that each be seen as one view of the same universe.

## Appendix A. Ledger of inputs and predictions

This appendix adds no new result. It gathers in one place what the sections above take from measurement, what they choose with today's data in view, and what they predict, so that a reader can see at once which agreements were possible only after the fact. Every number is rerun by `analysis/input_ledger_2026-11-18.py`, and the full table, with a column saying whether another published idea gives the same number, is `docs/ledger/TRPG_Ledger.md` in the code archive.

**Labels.** *Input*: taken from measurement. *Fitted*: a choice made with today's data in view. *Derived*: follows from the rules and inputs and agrees with a measurement already made. *Predicted*: follows from the rules and inputs, but the deciding measurement has not returned; it is not 100% until it does. *Lead*: a relation with one named missing rule, outside the core. *Effective*: an effective number of the strong force, counted on neither side.

**A.1 The headline prediction.** Normal neutrino ordering, with m₁ = 0.36 meV, m₂ = 8.7 meV and m₃ = 50.1 to 50.3 meV, summing to 0.0592 to 0.0594 eV (Section 9). It uses only the 45° rule that gives the tau mass, applied with one negative root, and the two measured splittings. The same relation for neutrinos was proposed by Brannen (2006); the framework supplies a reason for it but not a different number, so this test can falsify both and cannot separate them. JUNO is expected to decide the ordering at 3σ after about six and a half years of running (JUNO Collaboration, 2024), and DESI, Euclid and the next CMB experiments approach the needed sensitivity to the sum. Inverted ordering, or a sum clearly outside 0.058 to 0.07 eV, would falsify it.

**A.2 The count.** Counted like for like, the Standard Model with gravity and cosmology takes 34 free numbers. The framework removes six: the tau mass (Koide, 1983; 1776.969 MeV against 1776.93 ± 0.09, +0.4σ) and the down quark (Gatto, Sartori and Tonin, 1968; m~s~/m~d~ = 19.9 against 20.4 ± 0.3, −1.7σ), which measurement has confirmed; and the Higgs mass, the lightest neutrino mass, θ₁₂ given θ₁₃ and the neutrino CP phase, which wait on measurements not yet made. The count is therefore 32 today and 28 if every pending measurement agrees; the figure of about 28 used above is that ceiling. If the strong coupling also lands at 0.1178, one force strength is saved as well, but only once the Higgs mass lands too, since both rest on the same fitted grain. Four choices were made with today's data in view: the grain as the Planck energy divided by √2 (one of five equally natural readings); the weight rule of the far-side weak pairs; TM1 rather than another mixing pattern; and the sign pattern (−, +, +) of the neutrino roots. The containment pressure B and the stiffness of the containment field are effective numbers of the strong force and are counted on neither side.

**A.3 Labels tightened in gathering the ledger.** The lepton masses come from two numbers, the scale and the twist, with the 45° rule, not from one. The Cabibbo angle from the masses and the down quark from the Cabibbo angle are one relation and are counted once. Light bending, the black-hole ringdown and the age of the universe are standard results reproduced from measured inputs and are not counted as predictions. The neutron-star mass window of 2.0 to 2.15 solar masses is the observation the pairing gap must respect; the gap is the prediction. The Higgs-mass rule of Shaposhnikov and Wetterich fails with the Standard Model's states alone (2.4σ, Section 10) and passes at 1.3σ with the far-side states (Section 9), while needing a top mass 3.2σ below today's on the top mass alone.

**A.4 The framework is false if** any of the following is measured. Inverted neutrino ordering (JUNO, early 2030s; DUNE and Hyper-Kamiokande). A neutrino mass sum clearly outside 0.058 to 0.07 eV (DESI, Euclid). The strong coupling more than 3σ from 0.1178 in a lattice determination at about 0.3%. A top-quark mass that puts the Higgs rule more than 3σ off. The neutrino CP phase firmly outside 253–292° (DUNE, Hyper-Kamiokande). A quark pairing gap firmly outside 82–97 MeV, or a neutron star clearly above about 2.15 solar masses. Any proton decay (Hyper-Kamiokande). A fourth generation, a new force carrier, mirror particles or a second Higgs pair. Primordial gravitational waves (Simons Observatory, LiteBIRD). Neutrinos proven to be Dirac particles. The tau mass firmly away from 1776.969 MeV (Belle II, BESIII). Two leads would fail on their own without touching the core: the small neutrino splitting outside 7.32 to 7.48 × 10⁻⁵ eV² at JUNO's full precision (today's fits sit +0.7 to +1.1σ above the predicted 7.40), and the quark triangle angle γ firmly outside 64.1–67.6° (LHCb, Belle II), which follows from the lead α = 90° (compare Antusch, King, Malinsky and Spinrath, 2010).
