# The Recipe That Works: the Standard Model, Einstein's Gravity and the Story of the Cosmos

*The theory every other theory must match. It is not a theory of everything, but it is the best-tested description of nature ever written.*

---

## Chapter 1. A kitchen with seventeen ingredients

Imagine walking into a kitchen and finding a recipe card pinned to the wall. It lists seventeen ingredients. Follow it exactly and you can bake anything in the universe: a star, a grain of sand, a child. The card does not say why these seventeen. It does not say why the sugar weighs what it weighs. It simply works, every time, to more decimal places than any recipe has a right to.

That card is the **Standard Model of particle physics**. Its ingredients are the particles: six kinds of quark, six kinds of lepton (the electron and its relatives, including the ghostly neutrinos), the carriers of three forces (the photon for electricity and magnetism, the W and Z for the weak force, eight gluons for the strong force), and one more, the Higgs boson, found in 2012.

Pinned beside it is a second card, written by **Albert Einstein** in 1915. It says gravity is not a pull at all. Mass bends space and time, and things move along the bends. A bowling ball on a trampoline makes a dip, and marbles roll toward it.

A third card tells the story of the whole universe. It began hot and dense about 13.8 billion years ago, has been expanding ever since, and today is mostly made of two things nobody has seen: **dark matter** (about five times more than ordinary matter) and **dark energy** (which makes the expansion speed up). Physicists call this card **ΛCDM**.

## Chapter 2. How the recipe was written

Nobody wrote the recipe in one sitting. It was pieced together over half a century.

In the 1960s **Sheldon Glashow**, **Steven Weinberg** and **Abdus Salam** showed that electricity, magnetism and the weak force (the one that makes some atoms fall apart) are two faces of one force. **Peter Higgs**, **François Englert** and **Robert Brout** explained how particles get mass: space is filled with an invisible field, and particles that feel it are slowed, like a swimmer wading through honey. **Gerard 't Hooft** and **Martinus Veltman** proved the mathematics would not blow up. **David Gross**, **Frank Wilczek** and **David Politzer** showed why quarks are locked inside protons: the strong force grows stronger the further you pull, like a rubber band.

Each piece made predictions before experiments confirmed them. The W and Z particles were predicted, then found in 1983. The top quark was predicted, then found in 1995. The Higgs was predicted in 1964 and found in 2012. Einstein's gravity predicted that starlight bends around the Sun (seen in 1919) and that colliding black holes shake space itself (heard by LIGO in 2015).

## Chapter 3. Why it is so trusted

The electron behaves like a tiny magnet. The Standard Model predicts the strength of that magnet, and measurement agrees to about one part in a trillion. That is like measuring the distance from New York to Los Angeles and being right to the width of a human hair.

No other description of nature has been tested so hard and survived.

## Chapter 4. The cracks in the cake

The recipe has four known cracks.

1. **It does not say why.** The seventeen particles come with about 19 numbers (their masses, the strengths of the forces, how they mix) that must be measured and typed in. Add the neutrino masses and mixings, gravity's strength and the six numbers of the cosmic story, and you reach about **34 measured numbers**.
2. **Gravity and quantum physics do not fit together.** Einstein's smooth, bending space and the jittery quantum world give nonsense when you push them to the tiniest scales, inside black holes or at the first instant of time.
3. **Dark matter and dark energy are names, not explanations.**
4. **There should be no matter at all.** The recipe makes matter and antimatter in equal amounts, and they would destroy each other. Yet here we are, made of the leftovers.

## Chapter 5. What it means for everything else

Every theory of everything, TRPG included, must start by agreeing with this recipe wherever it has been tested. A new theory can explain the recipe, extend it, or reveal why its numbers are what they are. It cannot contradict it where it works.

### The theory at a glance
| | |
|---|---|
| **Main idea** | Particles and three forces as quantum fields; gravity as curved space-time; a hot, expanding universe |
| **Measured numbers it needs** | about 34 (19 particle numbers, 7–9 for neutrinos, gravity's strength, 6 for the cosmos) |
| **Confirmed predictions** | thousands: W, Z, top quark, Higgs, gravitational waves, the afterglow of the Big Bang |
| **Peer-reviewed?** | for fifty years |
| **What it has not explained** | why its numbers are what they are; dark matter; dark energy; the matter excess; quantum gravity |

### How it relates to TRPG
TRPG does not replace this recipe. It sits on top of it, like Einstein's gravity sits on top of Newton's. TRPG uses the Standard Model's own methods for its calculations and tries to explain some of its numbers (the tau mass, the down and up quarks, the lightest neutrino) and why the forces are the ones we see. **If TRPG is wrong, this recipe is still right.**
