# The Universe as a Computer Program: the Story of the Wolfram Physics Project

*The idea that underneath everything is a simple rule, applied over and over to a web of connections.*

---

## Chapter 1. Simple rules, complicated results

In the 1980s **Stephen Wolfram**, a physicist who went on to build a famous piece of mathematical software, became fascinated by a simple game. Take a row of black and white squares. Make a new row underneath using a rule that looks only at each square and its two neighbours. Repeat.

Some rules make dull stripes. Some make the same pattern forever. But one rule, which he called **Rule 30**, makes something that looks random, like the pattern on a seashell, from the simplest possible start. His conclusion, in his 2002 book *A New Kind of Science*: very simple rules can produce endless complexity. Perhaps the universe is one of them.

## Chapter 2. The web

In 2020 Wolfram, with **Jonathan Gorard** and **Max Piskunov**, launched the **Wolfram Physics Project**. In their picture, the universe is a giant web of points joined by links, called a **hypergraph**. A rule rewrites small pieces of the web, again and again, everywhere at once.

- **Space** is what the web looks like when you zoom out, the way a fishing net looks like a smooth sheet from far away.
- **Time** is the rewriting itself.
- **Energy and matter** are knots and tangles in the web that keep their shape as the rewriting goes on.

## Chapter 3. Many histories at once

Often a rule can be applied in several places, so the universe can branch into many possible histories. Wolfram's team calls the collection of all these histories the **multiway system**. They argue that quantum physics, with its many possibilities, is what it feels like to be inside such a branching web. They also argue that, in suitable limits, the web obeys equations like Einstein's.

## Chapter 4. What is missing

There are countless possible rules, and nobody yet knows which one is ours. Without the rule, the project cannot calculate the electron's mass, the strength of any force, or any number we have measured. Its results so far are about general behaviour, not specific numbers. Many physicists have criticised the project for being announced mainly outside the peer-reviewed journals, although Gorard has published technical papers.

### The theory at a glance
| | |
|---|---|
| **Main idea** | The universe is a web rewritten by one simple rule; space, time and matter emerge from it |
| **New ingredients** | the rule, which is not yet known |
| **Got right** | general links between rewriting webs and both relativity and quantum ideas (claimed) |
| **Not yet** | the rule itself; any measured number; wide peer review |

### How it differs from TRPG
- **The shared picture.** Both think in terms of connections rather than things. Wolfram's web of links is a cousin of TRPG's matrix of relationships.
- **The difference.** TRPG names its rules and puts numbers on the line that can fail. The Wolfram project has not yet found its rule, so it has no numbers to test.
- **Under peer review.** As a physics theory, the Wolfram project would not yet pass review on predictions. TRPG would face hard questions too, but it has testable numbers.
